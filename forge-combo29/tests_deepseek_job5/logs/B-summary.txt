JOB 5 PART B: does a stale table entry change a RESULT?

Target: 2Q3KR/4k3/8/8/8/8/8/8 w - - 0 1, go depth 12, Threads 1, Hash 16, built-in net.
Truth: mate in 2 moves (3 plies), line Qc6 Kd8 Kf7#.

B1. Fresh process, three times: mate 4, mate 4, mate 4  (stable)
    One earlier search of 8/8/8/1K6/8/8/1k6/2R1R3 w - - 0 1 at depth 12 -> target reports mate 2.
    So ONE earlier search is enough, and in this case it makes the answer CORRECT.

B1a. 35 of 40 candidate earlier positions change the target's score. Distribution:
     mate 2: 4 cases, mate 3: 20, mate 5: 9, mate 7: 1, cp 1637: 1, unchanged: 5
     (the truth is mate 2, so most of these make the answer WORSE, not better)

B1b. The trigger depends on the earlier search's depth, non-monotonically.
     witness searched at depth 1 -> target mate 4 (unchanged)
     witness at depth 2         -> target mate 3
     witness at depth 4, 6, 8   -> target mate 4 (unchanged)
     witness at depth 12        -> target mate 2

B1c. Hash size does not matter: 1, 4, 16, 64 and 256 MB all behave the same
     (fresh mate 4, after the witness mate 2). So it is not index pressure or a
     small-table collision.

B2. Instrumented probe (private copy, trace_patch.txt adds a print at every TT hit):
    second search (target at depth 12) after the witness: 17,575 TT hits,
    6,924 distinct keys, of which 0 were written during the first search.
    The hit entries carry depth=0, bounds 1/2, scores +-1000..1300 cp: these are
    QUIESCENCE entries, and they were written during the target's own search.

    The two roots share NO position: exhaustive trees to 6 plies have 0 common
    positions (70,138 vs 64,071 nodes). Their keys differ in all 32 low bits
    (b6f0e35d vs feca1fb4), so the two ROOTS do not collide either.

B3. Therefore the differing score is NOT explained by a transposition of a stored
    entry from the first search: nothing from the first search is ever read.
    What differs is the state the second search starts from. What I can show is
    that the second search's own quiescence entries are being reused across its
    own nodes (17,575 hits, all self-written), and that its result changes when
    the table starts non-empty instead of empty. Why a self-written depth-0 entry
    should flip a mate distance is NOT established.

    No claim about harm: I did not find a search that returns a wrong result in
    the sense of a losing move where a winning one exists. Every case above is a
    mate DISTANCE (or a cp score where a mate exists), not a reversed verdict.
