THE QUESTION: under S14 a history drop whose margin lies further below the threshold is regretted MORE
often when searched anyway (the rate rises with k). A margin should work the other way.

My prediction, before any split is counted:
1. The rise is a NODE-MIX effect: the moves that survive the wider margins (the k >= 1.5 classes) sit in
   nodes where the base regret rate is higher - I expect the (static eval - alpha) classes near 0 and the
   expected-cut-node kind to carry them. WITHIN one (eval - alpha) class the rate should be flat or
   falling with k.
2. The futility comparison behaves (its rate falls with k) because its margin scales with lmr_depth
   (the node's depth), so its k class is NOT a node-mix marker the way history's is.
3. I expect a high-rate history class above 250/1,000 to exist at node depth 1 (the (eval-alpha) >= 0
   or the cut-node kind) and a safe class under 30/1,000 among the deep (eval-alpha) cells.
If the within-class rates still rise with k, the margin genuinely selects worse moves backwards and the
history term's direction is the finding.
