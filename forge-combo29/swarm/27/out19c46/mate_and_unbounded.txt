`go mate N` IS A DEPTH CAP OF 2N-1, AND `movestogo`/`ponder` WITHOUT A CLOCK ARE UNBOUNDED
PC r15 068f0927, Threads 1, Hash 16, OwnBook false, fresh process per row, drain-based reader

=== A. `go mate N` on startpos: last depth reached vs 2N-1 ===
  N   bestmove   last depth   2N-1   matches
  1   d2d4       1            1      YES
  2   g1f3       3            3      YES
  3   d2d4       5            5      YES
  4   e2e4       7            7      YES
  5   e2e4       9            9      YES
  (N=6 and N=8 did not return inside the 4s cap on startpos - depth 9 is already slow there)

  Source agrees: `else if (token == "mate") limits.mate_moves = std::clamp(read_int(), 0, 32);`
  and the search turns mate_moves into depth 2N-1. So `go mate N` is NOT "search until you find a
  mate in N"; it is a DEPTH BOUND. A caller expecting mate-search semantics gets a truncated search.

=== B. CONTROL: the same command on a board where the mate is already visible ===
  mate 1  -> a1a8  depth 1
  mate 2  -> a1a8  depth 1
  mate 5  -> a1a8  depth 1
  mate 10 -> a1a8  depth 1
  The depth stays 1 because the mate is found immediately - which is why the startpos ladder, not
  this control, is what reveals the 2N-1 rule. **A control can be too easy to show the mechanism.**

=== C. `movestogo` and `ponder` WITHOUT a clock are UNBOUNDED (20s cap, no bestmove) ===
  go movestogo 20             -> NO BESTMOVE after 20s, reached depth 28
  go ponder                   -> NO BESTMOVE after 20s, reached depth 28
  go wtime 5000 movestogo 20  -> c2c4 after 20s (the clock bounds it)

  Source: `movestogo` only sets `limits.moves_to_go`, which the clock planner uses; with no
  `time_ms` there is no clock to plan against, so no limit is installed. `ponder` likewise sets a
  flag that only suppresses the clock (`!limits.infinite && !limits.ponder`). **Neither is a limit
  on its own, and neither says so.**

  CONSEQUENCE FOR A MATCH HARNESS: sending `go ponder` (or `movestogo` without a clock) yields a
  search that never returns. That is the same failure family as S-C26 (an absent `btime` giving no
  clock at all) and the same remedy: never rely on a `go` form whose limit is conditional.
