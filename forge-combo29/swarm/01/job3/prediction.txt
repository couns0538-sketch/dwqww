# PREDICTION — area 1, job 3 (`position startpos moves` with long games and one illegal move)

Committed and pushed ALONE before the first measuring command of job 3.

## What the job will do
1. Play a random legal game with python-chess (SEED 101) that is *stopped before any game end*: when the
   next move would end the game (checkmate/stalemate/insufficient material/75-move/5-fold) I take back to
   the last position that still had moves, so every ply sent is legal and the game never ended. Feed all
   1,000+ plies to the engine as `position startpos moves <all plies>`, then `go perft 1`, and compare the
   result with python-chess's legal move count for the same board.
2. The same game with ONE illegal move injected at ply 1, at ply 10 and at ply 500 (three separate runs of a
   fresh engine). Record the answer lines and which position the engine keeps (`go perft 1`).
3. Known reference: an illegal move keeps the legal prefix (perft 1 = 29 after e2e4 e7e5 e1e3).

## Numbers I predict (each with the range that kills it)

P1. The 1,000-ply move list is ACCEPTED with no error line, and perft 1 equals python-chess's legal move
    count for the final board. Any mismatch kills it (and would be a serious bug).
P2. Illegal move at ply 1: answered `info string illegal move <m>` and the engine keeps the START position,
    perft 1 = 20. Not 20 → kills it.
P3. Illegal move at ply 10: engine keeps the first 10 plies, perft 1 equals python-chess's count for the
    board after 10 plies. Any other count kills it.
P4. Illegal move at ply 500: engine keeps the first 500 plies, perft 1 equals python-chess's count after
    500 plies. Any other count kills it.
P5. Sanitizer lines (ASan+UBSan): 0 for every case above. One line kills it.
P6. No crash in any case.
P7. The illegal-move answer is a single `info string illegal move <token>` line, with no other error text,
    and it comes BEFORE `readyok`. Any extra error line kills it.

## Why
`apply_position` (src/uci.cpp 27-72) builds a *copy* of the board, applies the FEN or startpos, then applies
each move in turn; on a parse failure it prints `info string illegal move <token>`, assigns the copy to the
live board and returns. So the kept position should be exactly the legal prefix. The 1,000-ply case is the
interesting one: `key_history_` grows with every move and the repetition detector walks it, so a long game is
where an overflow or a quadratic blow-up would show.
