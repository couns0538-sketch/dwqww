# PREDICTION — tighter variant of the fifty-move patch (job 2b)

Committed and pushed ALONE before the first measuring command.

## Why a second variant
My first patch (job2/fifty_move_mate.patch.txt) removes `board.halfmove_clock() >= 100` from `root_drawn`
entirely. It is regression-tested and safe, but it makes EVERY halfmove>=100 root run a real search — including
the large family where the side to move has a bare king, where no mate is possible and the fast answer was
correct. This variant keeps the shortcut for exactly the cases where no mate can exist:

    const bool root_drawn = !legal.empty() &&
        (board.is_repetition() ||
         (board.halfmove_clock() >= 100 && !board.has_non_pawn_material(board.side_to_move())) ||
         board.is_insufficient_material());

So a halfmove>=100 root is adjudicated only when the side to move has NO non-pawn material (with a lone king
no mate is possible), and otherwise searched.

## Numbers I predict (the range that kills each)

P1. Mate-in-one board at halfmove 100: `mate 1` / bestmove a1a8, exactly as variant 1. Not mate → kills it.
P2. KQ v K and KR v K at halfmove 100/150 (`go depth 10`): score `cp 0` (the fifty-move draw is still seen).
    A non-zero score would mean the variant searches the draw away and is worse than variant 1.
P3. K v K at halfmove 150: still 0 nodes (the shortcut is kept where it is right). More than 0 nodes → the
    refinement did not take effect.
P4. Node cost on the KQ v K / KR v K roots: LOWER than variant 1 on at least one board, because those roots
    still pay a search; my expectation is 200–1,800 nodes, same order as variant 1.
P5. The four honesty pins unchanged. Any pin moving kills it.
P6. Applies with `patch -p2 --fuzz=0` to a clean 6b98f217 tree.
