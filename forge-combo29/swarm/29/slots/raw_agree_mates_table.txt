=== Does 29-F45's flatness hold on a SECOND, very different distribution? (mate-heavy set) ===
29-F45 measured S14-vs-PC20 bestmove agreement flat across depth on random-game positions (87.5/87.5/85.0/92.5 %).
A flat result on one distribution could still be that distribution's accident, so this repeats it on the mate-heavy
set from 29-F46 (40 positions, every one with a mate score at depth 12 under S14) -- a completely different
material and tactical profile.

PC r15 build, Threads 1, Hash 16, fresh process per search.

| depth | positions | same move | share | (random games, 29-F45) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 40 | 33 | 82.5 % | 87.5 % |
| 10 | 40 | 34 | 85.0 % | 87.5 % |
| 12 | 40 | 34 | 85.0 % | 85.0 % |
| 14 | 40 | 36 | 90.0 % | 92.5 % |

RESULT: agreement is again FLAT and at a very similar level (82.5-90 % here against 85-92.5 % there). Two very
different distributions give the same picture, so the flatness is not an accident of my random-game set.

What this does and does not do for 29-F45:
- DOES strengthen it: a second distribution, deliberately chosen to be unlike the first (bare-king endings with heavy
  material), reproduces both the level and the absence of a trend.
- Does NOT confirm it: this is still my harness, my binary, my sets. Confirmation needs a second SEAT, which is why
  29-N28 lists F42/F44/F45 as open and `REPRODUCE_pc20_quality.md` exists.
- Does not touch 29-F44 (the quality dead heat) -- that claim is about DISAGREEMENTS, and these two runs only measure
  their FREQUENCY.

Caveat: 40 positions per depth again, so ~+-5 points per share; the flatness across 8-14, and its similarity across
two distributions, is the claim.
