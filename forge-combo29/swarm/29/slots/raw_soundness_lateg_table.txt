=== Does 29-F44's quality DEAD HEAT hold on late-G, the one set where agreement trends? ===
29-F48 found move agreement is set-dependent (flat on R_2911 and my mate-heavy set, +15 pt upward on the last 40 of
G). 29-F44 claims the disagreements are of COMPARABLE quality (7-7-3 on R_2911). If the late-G disagreements were
mostly PC20 being WORSE, that would be the set where PC20's pruning actually costs — so this is the most demanding
place to test F44.

PC r15 build, 20 positions from the last 40 of G (seat 07's set), depth 10, Threads 1, Hash 16. Each disagreement
judged under the NEUTRAL S14 regime at depth 13; mover's advantage = negated engine score.

| result | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| SAME bestmove | 15 | 75.0 % |
| DIFFERENT bestmove | 5 | 25.0 % |

The five disagreements:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| 9 | c4e3 cp -720 | c4a5 cp -706 | -875 | -926 | S14 |

Tally: **PC20 better 2, S14 better 2, tie 1** -- a dead heat again.

RESULT: 29-F44's quality conclusion survives on the distribution where 29-F48 shows agreement is NOT flat. So:
- agreement FREQUENCY is set-dependent (29-F48);
- disagreement QUALITY is a dead heat on both R_2911 (7-7-3 over 17) and late G (2-2-1 over 5).
That is a coherent pair of statements: PC20 reaches a different move more often on some sets, and the moves it reaches
are of comparable value wherever I have measured.

CAVEAT, and it bites harder here: 2-2-1 over FIVE disagreements is far too small to establish a dead heat. It is
consistent with one; it does not show one. What it does show is the absence of any sign of PC20 being worse on the
set most likely to expose it. A confident claim needs many more late-G positions than 20.
