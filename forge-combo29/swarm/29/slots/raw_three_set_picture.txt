WHAT THIS MEANS, and it corrects my own 29-F45 rather than extending it:
- 29-F45 said agreement is flat and therefore disagreements are not horizon noise. That is TRUE ON R_2911 — and
  seat 07 reproduced my four figures EXACTLY (87.5 / 87.5 / 85.0 / 92.5 %), so the measurement is sound.
- But it is NOT true in general: on the last 40 of G agreement climbs 55 -> 77.5 % from depth 8 to 12, a 15-point
  upward trend with a 22.5-point spread. An upward trend is EXACTLY the signature of horizon artefacts.
- So the correct claim is SET-DEPENDENT: **on some distributions the disagreements are genuine preference
  differences at every depth (R_2911, my mate-heavy set); on others a substantial share are shallow-horizon
  artefacts that dissipate with depth (late G).**
- 29-F47's "two different distributions confirm the flatness" was right about those two sets and wrong to imply
  generality. Seat 07 found the third set that breaks it, which is more valuable than another flat one.

STATE: 29-F45 is now **CONFIRMED ON ITS OWN SET by seat 07 (07-C56), and its generalisation is REFUTED BY the same
check** — the set-dependence is the corrected finding. This is the same fault shape as 29-F38/39, 29-F40/41, 29-F41/42
and 29-F43/44: a true measurement presented as a general law, fixed by varying the axis that matters (here, the set).

Note on method: seat 07 explicitly ran my ORIGINAL sample before judging, which is why the report is "confirmed on its
set, rate is set-dependent" rather than a bare REFUTES. Their note says a bare REFUTE would have been procedurally

correcting a measurement or bounding a generalisation.
