# ADDENDUM — variant H (generator emission order) is REFUTED; and the lone-king defect is a misevaluation

## A. Variant H: canonicalising the generator's emission order does NOT fix the asymmetry

The one-position dump in `REPORT_cause.md` showed the move generator's emission order differs under a colour
mirror. Variant H (`make_variant_h.py`, patch `FORGE_swarm06_generator_order.patch.txt`, applies `-p1` no fuzz)
reorders the finished move list by the mirror-invariant key `(from^56, to^56, promotion)`.

**It works as designed**: measured on three positions, emission order becomes mirror-invariant on all three
(`variant_h_fixture.txt`: base False/False/False → variant H True/True/True), and **perft 5 is unchanged at
4,865,609**, which is the proof it is a pure reorder.

**And it changes nothing about the asymmetry:**

| build | depth-10 G bestmove mismatches | score differences |
|---|---|---|
| base | **101** of 353 | 334 |
| variant H (generator order canonical) | **101** of 353 | 338 |

**So the generator's emission order is not what produces the asymmetry — eighth mechanism refuted.** This is
consistent with the picker re-scoring every move before it is searched: the order the generator hands over is
discarded by the sort that follows, so making that order canonical cannot matter.

Honesty: variant H fails the pins (bench 11 367464, bench 13 1010323, bench 16 2834878; perft 5 unchanged),
as any order change must. Evidence only, not a candidate.

## B. The lone-king defect: a MISEVALUATION, now established by ground truth

My S-F32–S-F34 were withdrawn because I read the mate sign backwards (`mated_in(ply) = -MateValue + ply`, so a
longer mate is a better score). That withdrawal stands as a claim about *signs*.

But a play-out settles the position independently of any score, and **I reproduced it exactly** on the base
build (`playout_verify.py`, both sides played by the engine at depth 12, python-chess adjudicating, scores
never consulted, 3 runs each):

| White's first move | plies survived | end |
|---|---|---|
| c6d6 | **22, 22, 22** | checkmate each time |
| c6c7 | **20, 20, 20** | checkmate each time |

Fully deterministic, and `c6d6` survives **strictly longer in every run** (min 22 vs max 20). So:

- The engine's preference for `c6c7` **is wrong by ground truth**, regardless of any score or sign.
- The error is in its **score for `c6d6`** — it rates that move `mate -8` when playing it out actually holds
  22 plies. A misevaluation of the losing alternative, which then produces the wrong choice.
- It is **not** a cross-build regression (base shows it), and **not** a sign convention problem.

Recorded as S-F39. S-F32/S-F33/S-F34 stay withdrawn as *ordering-flaw* claims; the defect is real but its
nature is different from what I first reported.

## C. Why the scores could not settle any of this

S-F38, and this is the reusable lesson: a `searchmoves` score and a free-choice score at the same nominal depth
are **not comparable** — they search different trees. On this board the restricted searches reached
**seldepth 43 on 16M nodes** while the free choice reached **seldepth 37 on 166M**. Two numbers that look like
they should be comparable were not. Any claim of the form "it plays X but scores Y better" must quote seldepth
and nodes for both, and say whether each was restricted or free.

## D. The picker's root scores: identical at the root, diverging at iteration 4 (variant I)

Variant I (`make_variant_i.py`) prints the MovePicker's own score for every root move, before any subtree of
that iteration is searched. Instrumentation only: **pins unchanged** (312866 / 862329 / 3024920 / 4865609).

On the clearest mismatching pair `1r6/8/4K3/5p2/5k2/7p/8/8 w`, depth 6:

| iteration | root moves whose picker score differs from the mirror |
|---|---|
| 1 | **0 of 7** |
| 2 | **0 of 7** |
| 3 | **0 of 7** |
| **4** | **2 of 7** |
| 5–8 | 2 of 7 |

And the first iteration's actual vector, both sides, every entry 0:

```
e6d5:0 e6d6:0 e6d7:0 e6e5:0 e6e7:0 e6f6:0 e6f7:0
```

**So the history / threat / continuation tables are NOT the cause at the root.** At iteration 1 the tables are
empty, and the picker's scores are identical — every entry is 0 — and stay identical through iteration 3. The
first divergence appears at **iteration 4**, i.e. *after* the search has filled the tables, which means the
table divergence is a **consequence** of the two searches having gone different ways, not its cause.

That is the decisive disproof of my own last surviving hypothesis, and it is the ninth mechanism refuted. It
also closes the causal chain in a consistent direction: the root is identical, the first three iterations are
identical, and the paths separate only once the search itself has run — so the asymmetry is generated by the
search *between* the root and the tables, not by the tables as an input.

With a free-iteration search, iterations 1–3 agreeing is itself informative: at depth 3 the two searches are
still identical, matching the earlier depth curve where bestmove mirroring is 40/40 at depth 1 and degrades
from depth 2–3 onward.

Honesty: variant I is instrumentation only and **passes the pins exactly**, so unlike variants A–H it is not
disqualified as a build. It is still not a candidate for the repository (it adds an info line to UCI output).


### What this settles

By the brief's own branch rule: the picker's inputs are identical, the scores and seldepths at iteration 1 are
identical, and **the node counts already differ**. So the cause is **upstream of the picker entirely** — not in
the history/threat/continuation tables (they are all zero here), not in the sort, not in the generator's
emission order (variant H left the count at 101), and not in the root window or the evaluation (which is
symmetry-exact).

A 1-node difference at depth 1 with identical scores means the two searches **visited a different number of
nodes while reaching the same answer at the same depth**. At depth 1 the only things that can differ are the
number of moves tried and whether each gets a qsearch. This is the point to instrument next, and it is now a
single, named, small target rather than "the search".

### Correction to what I said a moment ago

My first `dump_picker` run reported "0 of 7 through iteration 3" and I described iteration 1's vector as all
zeros — that was right for the *first* root visit but the "iteration N" numbering in that table was my own
counter over dumps, not the engine's iteration, and on that run the first dump happened to be a later visit.
The numbers in this section are the corrected, properly-scoped ones (depth-1 search = exactly one root visit;
per-iteration node counts straight from the engine's own `info ... depth N` lines). Both readings agree on the
conclusion — the tables are empty and identical at the first root visit — but the earlier table's iteration
labels were not the engine's iterations and should not be quoted.
