# REPORT_job3 — seat 11, area 1: `position startpos moves`, 1,000 legal plies and one illegal move

## 1. What was asked
`position startpos moves` with 1,000 legal plies of a random game (the driver must not let the
game end: take back to the last position that still had moves), then the same list with one
illegal move at ply 1, 10 and 500: the answer lines and which position the engine keeps
(`go perft 1`). Known: an illegal move keeps the legal prefix (perft 1 = 29 after
e2e4 e7e5 e1e3).

## 2. The answer
All 7 cases agree with python-chess exactly, on the plain build and under ASan+UBSan.
The engine keeps exactly the legal prefix in every case, reports `info string illegal move
<token>`, and never crashes or emits a sanitizer line. The brief's known number reproduces
(perft 1 = 29 after e2e4 e7e5 e1e3).

One thing worth recording, and I got it wrong first: I wrote that "counting perft 1 gave 29 for
BOTH the full 1,000-ply list and the list-plus-illegal-move, which looks like the engine
ignored the illegal move". The coincidence I assumed does not exist — the 999-ply prefix has
**51** legal moves, not 29 (`job3/perft2_identity.txt`). The full list has 29 and the
full-list-plus-illegal-move has 29 because the engine kept the full list, exactly as required.
Perft 2 confirms it: full list 1,485, 999-ply prefix 1,427, full-list-plus-illegal 1,485.
So the perft-1 check was never ambiguous here; my concern was unfounded and the perft-2 test
adds nothing but confirmation.

## 3. How sure
High. Every case is one command, the raw answer lines are committed, and the "keeps the
prefix" conclusion was re-tested with perft 2 after the perft-1 count proved ambiguous.
The 1,000-ply list was generated by a committed script with seed 1101 and reaches 1,000 plies
with **0 backtracks**.

## 5. Findings
No engine defect. The behaviour matches every part of the brief's known line and is strictly
consistent under both builds.

- Prefix is kept exactly. Evidence `job3/rel/results.txt` and `job3/asan/results.txt`.
  Shows it again:
  ```
  python3 forge-combo29/swarm/11/job3/job3.py /home/ubuntu/builds/rel/forge \
      forge-combo29/swarm/11/job3/moves_1000.txt /tmp/j3
  ```
- A non-UCI token is reported the same way as an illegal move, not ignored:
  `info string illegal move zzzz` (case `garbage`). This is the same code path as the illegal
  move (`candidate.parse_uci(token)` returning nullopt, uci.cpp:61–66).
- The perft-1 check is sufficient here and I wrongly doubted it. The 999-ply prefix has 51
  legal moves, so "29 after the illegal move" can only mean the full list was kept
  (`job3/perft2_identity.txt`). I had written the opposite before measuring it, and the
  correction is recorded rather than quietly removed.

## 6. Suspicions, not proven
- I did not test a list longer than 1,000 plies; `parse_uci` per token is linear, so I expect
  no limit, but that is a guess.
- I did not test an illegal move that is syntactically a legal UCI string but of the wrong
  side (e.g. `e7e5` when it is White's turn) separately from `e1e3`; both are "not in the
  legal move list" so I expect the identical path.

## 7. What I did NOT do
- Did not test `position fen ... moves ...` (only `startpos moves`), which is job 1's FEN side.
- Did not test 1,000 plies ending in a position with zero legal moves (the driver forbids a
  game end by design, per the brief).
- Did not measure timing.

## 8. What I am NOT sure about
- Whether a *repetition-heavy* 1,000-ply list behaves the same. My generator allows repetition
  and reached 1,000 plies with 0 backtracks, so the list probably contains many; I did not
  count how many threefold positions it passed through.
