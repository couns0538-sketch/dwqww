# PREDICTION — HOW FAR should the self-play budget move up? (the numbers behind S-F31's advice)

S-F31 says a strength-minded self-play choice should move up from 2,000 nodes. It gives no target, which makes the
advice hard to act on. This job measures cost against budget for four budgets: 2000 (current), 8000, 20000 and
50000 — the last being the PC's smallest MATCH budget, so it is a budget the owner already uses elsewhere.

Method: 40 real-game positions, PC build under PC20, Threads 1, Hash 16. For each budget B and each position:
  move_B = bestmove at `go nodes B`
  cost(B) = (score after move_ref) - (score after move_B), both from the mover's point of view, judged by ONE
            fixed reference search (`go depth 16`, deeper than the depth-12 proxy S-F30 used, because S-F31 showed
            the magnitude grows with reference depth).
  move_ref = bestmove at `go nodes 250000` (the PC's largest match budget).
Reported per budget: same-move count vs ref, median cost, count worse by >30 and >100, and the sum of costs
(a single "total damage" figure, which is the number that matters if the budget is used for many moves).

P1. Cost falls MONOTONICALLY as the budget rises: predicted median cost and total damage both decrease from 2000
    through 8000, 20000 to 50000. Kills it: any budget whose total damage exceeds the previous one.
P2. 8000 nodes captures most of the gain: predicted the total damage at 8000 is less than half of that at 2000.
    Kills it: less than a quarter of the gain available at 8000 (then the useful step is larger).
P3. 50000 is close to the reference: predicted at most 2 of 40 positions worse by >100 cp, and a median of 0.
    Kills it: 5 or more >100 cp at 50000 (then the reference budget itself is not enough and I must say the
    "cost" measure is relative to a budget that is also imperfect).
P4. The number of positions where the move CHANGES from 2000 to 8000 is small — under a third — so most of the
    gain comes from a few positions improving a lot, not from everything shifting. Kills it: most positions
    changing, which would mean the budget affects nearly every decision.
