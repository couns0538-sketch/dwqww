# PREDICTION — job 3 follow-up: the mate-vs-cp pairs

In the depth-10 G run, 5 of 353 pairs had a MATE score on one side and a plain cp score on the other:
  idx 69  cp -2225 vs mate -10
  idx 184 cp -2394 vs mate -8
  idx 196 cp 4402  vs mate 14   (seat 06's S-F9/S-F11 also found this one and called it a depth artefact)
  idx 217 cp 2557  vs mate 11
and one in R: idx 120 cp 2417 vs mate 9.

Question: is the side reporting cp actually blind to the mate (a real asymmetric evaluation/mate-detection
fault), or is it a depth/order effect that any depth-limited search has?

Method: for each such position and its mirror, search at depths 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 12 and record the score.
If the cp side reports mate at some depth, the mate is there and the depth-10 cp was a depth artefact.
Also: same position, same depth, run TWICE in two fresh processes — if the two runs agree, the search is
deterministic and a mate/cp split is a property of the POSITION, not of run-to-run noise.

P1. The cp side reaches a mate score by depth 12. Kills it: still cp at depth 12 and not increasing.
P2. Same position at the same depth twice gives the identical score (deterministic, Threads 1). Kills it:
    any difference between the two identical runs (then the engine is not deterministic at Threads 1 and
    every comparison in this job needs repeated runs).
P3. At depth 1 and depth 2 the pair scores are exactly equal (mirror symmetry holds when there is nothing
    to reorder). Kills it: a difference at depth 1 (that would be a real evaluation/order bug).
