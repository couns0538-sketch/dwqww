# PREDICTION — the clock path across a SEQUENCE of moves in one process

All my clock work so far sent one `go` per process. The PC's matches keep ONE process for the whole game and
send `go wtime ... btime ...` with a decreasing clock. Things that can only go wrong there: the engine's own
bookkeeping of the clock across moves, and whether the increment accumulates.

P1. In one process, 10 successive `go` commands with a clock that DECREASES by the time used plus the increment
    each move all return a bestmove, and none exceeds the time it was told it had. Predicted: all 10 return, 0
    overruns. Kills it: any missing bestmove, or any run over its stated remaining time.
P2. The clock I pass is honoured as a DECREASING budget: predicted the engine's search time falls as the passed
    clock falls, i.e. time(move 10) < time(move 1) when I feed a deliberately shrinking clock. Kills it: the
    search time not falling when the clock is artificially reduced to a tenth.
P3. Giving the same clock value 10 times in a row gives the same behaviour on move 1 and move 10 (the engine does
    not accumulate state that changes its time management). Predicted the move may differ (the board changed), but
    the TIME USED should be within a factor of 2 between move 1 and move 10 at an identical clock.
    Kills it: time used growing or shrinking by more than 2x with an identical clock, which would mean cross-move
    time-manager state.
P4. `go movetime 100` interleaved with a clock search does not corrupt the next clock search (the engine does not
    keep the movetime limit). Predicted the following clock search behaves like a fresh one. Kills it: an
    overrun or a 100 ms-bounded search after the clock search.
