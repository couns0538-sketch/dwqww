# PREDICTION — are S-F30's largest costs real, or depth-12 proxy artefacts?

S-F30 measured the cost of the 2,000-node self-play move against the 250,000-node match move using a SINGLE
depth-12 reference search of each resulting position. That is a proxy: in a sharp or lost position a depth-12
score is not ground truth, and four of my "worse" cases and three "better" ones are within a range where the
proxy could be wrong.

Method: take the 8 positions where |cost| > 30 cp from S-F30 and re-judge each pair with DEEPER reference
searches (depths 14, 16, 18), independently of the low/ref budgets. For each position, for each candidate move,
search the resulting position once per depth and record the mover's score. The cost is then the difference
at each depth, so I can see whether the sign and size are stable as the reference deepens.

P1. The sign is STABLE for at least 6 of the 8 positions across depths 12/14/16/18. Kills it: fewer than 4
    (then S-F30's individual cases are proxy artefacts and only its aggregate "wash" claim survives).
P2. The magnitude SHRINKS with depth for most positions, as the deeper search finds resources for the losing
    side. Predicted the median |cost| at depth 18 is smaller than at depth 12. Kills it: growth, which would
    mean the shallow budget's choices get worse with more search (also worth knowing, in the other direction).
P3. idx 34's +109 cp (the largest loss) SURVIVES at depth 18 at more than 50 cp. Kills it: it falls below
    30 cp, in which case the single largest "loss" in S-F30 is an artefact and I must say so.
P4. idx 37's -109 cp (the largest GAIN, h8h1 winning a rook) SURVIVES at depth 18, because a won rook is a
    material fact that deeper search cannot take back. Kills it: it reverses, which would mean my hand-check
    at depth 14 was also wrong.
