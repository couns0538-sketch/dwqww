> **THE FIRST RULE: if the answer does not belong to the position, the position was not accepted.**> This engine **refuses** an invalid FEN and keeps the PREVIOUS board, so a harness that does not read
> the setup output confidently measures the wrong position and reports it as a result. The tell is in the
> answer itself: a move that could not occur on the board you sent means the board you sent was
> rejected. Mine gave `e2e4` for a position with a lone black king on h8 — impossible, so the FEN had
> been refused ("side that moved left its king in check") and STARTPOS was being searched. **Read every
> line between `position` and `readyok`, and sanity-check that the bestmove is legal on YOUR board**
> (07-C46). **COUNT, CORRECTED AFTER CHECKING: this happened to me ONCE in the record** (job47, 07-C46). I
> first wrote "three occurrences" and it was wrong — the `e2e4`-on-a-bare-king case IS the job47 case, so I
> counted one incident twice, and the other two I had in mind (the 2640-era board, position 155) were **not**
> refused FENs at all. Stated as one rather than three because an inflated count is exactly the kind of
> evidence I have been telling other seats to check. **Validate every FEN with python-chess before using it;
> that alone would have caught it.** **AND NOTE WHY `readyok` IS NOT ENOUGH: a refused position still answers
> `readyok`** (01-S-F94, confirmed in 07-C70), so "send isready and wait for readyok to know the position is set"
> proves nothing. The guard that survives a lying handshake is **`go perft 1` compared with python-chess for the
> intended board** — on a refused FEN it returns startpos's count against the intended one. The engine also prints
> `info string invalid FEN`, so reading setup output catches it; the perft comparison is stronger because it needs
> no trust in the engine's own message.

> **ASSERT YOUR HARNESS SETTINGS EXPLICITLY, AND RECORD THEM: `Threads 1`, `MultiPV 1`, `OwnBook false`.** Defaults are fine; a leftover non-default silently changes the measurement with no error and a plausible node count. `MultiPV 2` costs **3 plies on startpos at `go nodes 20000`** (d11 → d8) and 2 on a middlegame, monotone in MultiPV, and reproduced exactly in all 16 cells (07-C49). Node *stops* are deterministic here — every cell of that test matched across two runs — whereas node counts under a **clock** are not (07-C47), so quote a clock result by its move and wall time.

> **IF A DEFECT SHOULD VANISH WITH MORE BUDGET AND IT DOES NOT, SUSPECT THE CHECKER.** This is the cheapest tell I found: my PV-length checker called a **correct** 9-ply PV for `mate 5` truncated at **all three** budgets including 250,000 nodes, when the thing under test was a truncation that is supposed to disappear as the budget rises. A "defect" that does not improve with more nodes, more depth or more time is far more likely to be my arithmetic than the engine's (I had required `2|N|` plies for a positive mate instead of `2N−1`). Corollary: **an empty scan means scarcity, not absence** — only **2.2 %** of positions (8 of 361) report a mate at `go nodes 20000`, so a 120-position filter for long mates legitimately finds nothing, and a sample with no mate scores cannot measure mate-score truncation at all (07-C51).
> 
> **THE SAME SWEEP DECIDES WHICH, IN EITHER DIRECTION — pair this rule with 07-C53.**
> - A defect that **does not** vanish with more budget/depth/time is most likely **my checker being wrong**
>   (07-C51: `2|N|` instead of `2N−1` called a correct PV truncated at every budget).
> - A defect that **does** vanish with more depth is a **horizon artefact, not a defect** (07-C53: `f6e7` at
>   depth 20 becomes `f6f5` at depth 24 on `6k1/8/5K2/8/6r1/8/8/8 w`, so a harness testing only at match depth
>   would have filed a bug that does not exist).
> 
> So before reporting any defect: **sweep the budget or depth and see which way it moves.** It collapsing at
> higher effort means the engine was fine; it refusing to move means I am measuring my own arithmetic. Both
> answers are decided by one sweep that costs a few minutes, and both errors are avoided by it.

> **AN EXACT MATCH TO SOMEONE'S BASELINE IS THE FINGERPRINT OF HAVING CHANGED NOTHING.** Read this one before the mechanics below, because it is the part that survives this seat and this repo. I was replicating 16-S-F35 and my first run reproduced their baseline **to the digit** — 34/120 — while the treatment arm came out **identical**, case for case. That felt like two seats independently measuring the same thing, which is the most reassuring feeling available in this work. It was the exact opposite: **the treatment arm producing bit-identical output to the control means the treatment was not applied.** A confirmation and a null result are indistinguishable at the moment you read them, and only one of the two is what you think it is. **The general form: when your treatment arm matches your control arm exactly, do not conclude that the effect is absent — conclude that you have not yet performed the experiment.** A null from an unapplied treatment is worth nothing and, filed as a refutation, costs someone else their correct result and costs you your credibility on every later claim. Corollary for the control arm itself: matching the other seat's baseline is **not** a check on your own build, because the baseline is by construction the unmodified path — it will match whenever you have failed to change anything, which is precisely the case you need to detect. **Only a number the treatment must move can serve as that check.**

> **BEFORE REPORTING THAT A PATCH DID NOTHING, PROVE THE PATCH IS IN THE BINARY — AND PROVE IT WITH AN OBSERVABLE THE CODE PATH MUST MOVE.** A patch that ships its own switch (`#ifndef X / #define X 0`) is **not applied by applying and building it**; you compile the stock path and measure stock against stock. That is what happened to me on 16-S-F35: I got **34/120 vs 34/120**, no improvement, and nearly filed a REFUTATION of a claim that is in fact exactly right (rebuilding with `-DX=1` gives **120/120, residue zero**, 07-C74). **`perft` is the WRONG control for this class** — it is precisely what a move-order patch leaves alone, so it passes on an inert build and reassures falsely. The right control is the one that must move: **bench nodes went 312866 → 376974** (07-C74). **A refutation is a claim about someone else's work and carries the same evidence burden as a confirmation** — mine rested on evidence entirely silent about the thing under test.

> **BEFORE CITING A LIBRARY FUNCTION AS YOUR EVIDENCE, READ WHAT IT ACTUALLY COMPUTES.** I wrote that `python-chess`'s `is_fifty_moves()` was **False** on the boards that proved 07-F3 — twice, in two findings. It returns **True** there: its body is only `halfmove_clock >= n and any(self.generate_legal_moves())`, with **no** test for whether a capture or pawn move is available to reset the counter (07-C80). My measured numbers were all correct because my scripts used `is_fifty_moves() and not resets`, which is the real condition — **only my prose was wrong**, which is the more dangerous case, because the data survives scrutiny and the sentence does not. **A reader who checks your citation against the library will find the library disagreeing and will be right to distrust the rest.** Read the function body before you name it as the authority for a claim, and prefer stating the condition in full (`halfmove ≥ 100 AND no reset available`) over naming a function that only approximates it.

> **A CHANGE IS NOT VERIFIED UNTIL IT IS VERIFIED WHERE THE NEXT PERSON WILL APPLY IT.** Two of my near-misses tonight were the same shape, and neither was about the content of the change. **(1)** A patch whose switch defaults to 0 is applied and built and measures stock against stock (07-C74). **(2)** A patch diffed against **my own already-patched working copy** applies cleanly for me and fails `Hunk #1` on a pristine tree — v2 of my own 07-F1 patch, content correct and still not shippable (07-C79). **Generating a deliverable by diffing your working copy is how this gap is created**, because your working copy is the one place the patch is guaranteed to apply. **The test that catches both: apply from a clean checkout of the stated base, and rebuild from scratch — the binary should be byte-identical to the one your evidence came from.** If it differs, either the patch is incomplete or the tree was not what you said. That byte-identity is not a formality; it is the proof that the patch alone determines the build and that nothing in your scratch environment is load-bearing.

> **WHEN THE COMPLAINT IS "THIS NUMBER IS A LIE", THE FIX MUST MAKE THE NUMBER TRUE — NOT JUST RARER.** My own 07-F1 patch cut **64** fabricated `score cp 0 nodes 0` lines down to **1** and I nearly shipped it on that count. The surviving line read `info depth 64 … nodes 0` for a `go searchmoves` with no depth: **the identical self-contradiction — a completed depth claimed by a search that never ran — printed once instead of 64 times** (07-C78). A count-based check cannot see this, because the count went the right way. **When a defect is "the output asserts something false", the pass condition is that the surviving output is TRUE, so read its content, not its multiplicity.** Generalises past this repo: reducing how often a wrong thing is said is not the same as making it right, and a metric that improves in the right direction can be concealing an unchanged fault.

> **BEFORE CALLING SOMEONE'S NUMBER WRONG, RUN IT ON THEIR OWN SAMPLE.** A number can be **right on its own
> sample and wrong as a generalisation**, and those are different faults needing different reports. My G sample put
> 29-F45 **30 points** away from their published figures — far outside the ±5-point sampling error, and their own
> recipe invites a `REFUTES` line — so filing REFUTES would have been procedurally correct and **wrong**. Running
> their **own R_2911 set** reproduced all four numbers **to the digit** (87.5 / 87.5 / 85.0 / 92.5 %) and showed the
> difference was the **set**, not their measurement (07-C56). The correct report was "confirmed on its set, rate is
> set-dependent", which is more useful than either a bare CONFIRMS or a bare REFUTES. So: reproduce on the original
> sample first; only then decide whether you are correcting a measurement or bounding a generalisation.

> **For a side-to-move-relative score under a colour mirror, the invariant is EQUALITY, not
> negation.** `Board.mirror()` swaps colours *and* the side to move, so a +255 White advantage reads
> +255 for Black in the mirror. **The tell that catches a wrong checker: its "asymmetric" pairs share
> the same sign** (`orig cp −304` against `mirror cp −301`) — a negation-based checker also reports
> *fewer* symmetries, and its failures carry the **correct mirrored move**. Mine reported 4/120
> instead of 7/120 that way, and seat 16 had already documented the trap in its C5.

> **A damage table must exclude incomparable pairs.** Ranking positions by "score of the small budget's
> move minus score of the large budget's move" is only valid when both are centipawns. **Comparing a
> mate score against a centipawn loss is not a preference** — `mate −12` against `cp −2196` is two
> different losses, and subtracting them invents ~9,800 cp of damage. My run's largest gap, 95,212 cp,
> was exactly that artefact; it would have been the headline of any damage table I published (07-C44).

> **A small-sample rate does not order anything.** Two disjoint 100-position samples flipped the
> ordering of two node budgets ten points apart; over all 353 positions they were indistinguishable
> (07-C42/C43). Report a rate with its sample size, and say whether it ranks.

> **A node count under a clock stop is not reproducible until you prove it is.** Repeat the SAME cell
> on the SAME binary and see whether it reproduces before comparing anything across builds. Mine did
> not: at `go wtime 11 btime 11` one binary gave **412 nodes in one invocation and 971 in five others**,
> and 4 of 15 of my own sweep values failed their own repeats (07-C47). This applies to **every**
> node-count table taken under a wall-clock stop, **including my own earlier ones** — `job11`'s clock
> tables record a `nodes` column, and although I used it only as a parse check ("rows without nodes"),
> not as a cross-build comparison, any reader comparing those numbers should not. Under a clock, compare
> the **move** (discrete, reproducible) and the **wall time**, not the node count.
