## A harness error of mine, recorded per the coordinator's instruction (same class as the rest of the night)

In job31 I built a boundary test for 07-F1 — "does the early branch fire when `searchmoves` leaves
exactly ONE candidate?" — and my first version used

    position startpos moves e2e4 e7e5 g1f3
    go depth 6 searchmoves f3e5

The engine answered `0000` with 6 zero-node depth lines on the base build, and my harness printed a
warning that read like a finding: *"a zero-node answer on a ONE-candidate legal restriction -> the
early branch is taken even when a legal candidate exists"*. That would have been a real and serious
find. It was **false**: after `e2e4 e7e5 g1f3` it is **Black** to move, so `f3e5` was never a legal
move for the side to move and the engine was correctly reporting an empty root.

python-chess showed it in one line (`is f3e5 legal? False`, piece on f3 is `N`, piece on e5 is `p`),
which is the habit that has caught every error of mine tonight: check the input against an independent
oracle before believing the tool. Corrected case (`... g1f3 b8c6`, White to move, Nxe5 a real
capture): base `f3e5` 138 nodes, PC20 `f3e5` 175 nodes — the boundary is fine (07-C25).

The reason I am writing this as a FINDINGS paragraph rather than silently fixing the script: it is the
same failure shape as the five blind controls (the go/stop cycle, eval symmetry, the SEE oracle, the
option surface, the UBSan overflow), and as the 0-node harness bug seat 19 relayed. In every case the
instrument, not the engine, was wrong, and in every case what caught it was a check that could say
"no". A warning printed by a harness that has not validated its own input is not a finding.
