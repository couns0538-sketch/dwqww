**Summary for a reader with one minute.** This seat's net result is **one real engine defect that

**Rules that will save the next person more time than any individual result here. The first one is the
cheapest to apply and caught me three separate times tonight:**

> **THE FIRST RULE: if the answer does not belong to the position, the position was not accepted.**> This engine **refuses** an invalid FEN and keeps the PREVIOUS board, so a harness that does not read
> the setup output confidently measures the wrong position and reports it as a result. The tell is in the
> answer itself: a move that could not occur on the board you sent means the board you sent was
> rejected. Mine gave `e2e4` for a position with a lone black king on h8 — impossible, so the FEN had
> been refused ("side that moved left its king in check") and STARTPOS was being searched. **Read every
> line between `position` and `readyok`, and sanity-check that the bestmove is legal on YOUR board**
> (07-C46). Validate every FEN with python-chess before using it; two of my three occurrences would have
> been caught by that alone.

> **ASSERT YOUR HARNESS SETTINGS EXPLICITLY, AND RECORD THEM: `Threads 1`, `MultiPV 1`, `OwnBook false`.** Defaults are fine; a leftover non-default silently changes the measurement with no error and a plausible node count. `MultiPV 2` costs **3 plies on startpos at `go nodes 20000`** (d11 → d8) and 2 on a middlegame, monotone in MultiPV, and reproduced exactly in all 16 cells (07-C49). Node *stops* are deterministic here — every cell of that test matched across two runs — whereas node counts under a **clock** are not (07-C47), so quote a clock result by its move and wall time.

> **IF A DEFECT SHOULD VANISH WITH MORE BUDGET AND IT DOES NOT, SUSPECT THE CHECKER.** This is the cheapest tell I found: my PV-length checker called a **correct** 9-ply PV for `mate 5` truncated at **all three** budgets including 250,000 nodes, when the thing under test was a truncation that is supposed to disappear as the budget rises. A "defect" that does not improve with more nodes, more depth or more time is far more likely to be my arithmetic than the engine's (I had required `2|N|` plies for a positive mate instead of `2N−1`). Corollary: **an empty scan means scarcity, not absence** — only **2.2 %** of positions (8 of 361) report a mate at `go nodes 20000`, so a 120-position filter for long mates legitimately finds nothing, and a sample with no mate scores cannot measure mate-score truncation at all (07-C51).
> 
> **THE SAME SWEEP DECIDES WHICH, IN EITHER DIRECTION — pair this rule with 07-C53.**
> - A defect that **does not** vanish with more budget/depth/time is most likely **my checker being wrong**
>   (07-C51: `2|N|` instead of `2N−1` called a correct PV truncated at every budget).
> - A defect that **does** vanish with more depth is a **horizon artefact, not a defect** (07-C53: `f6e7` at
>   depth 20 becomes `f6f5` at depth 24 on `6k1/8/5K2/8/6r1/8/8/8 w`, so a harness testing only at match depth
>   would have filed a bug that does not exist).
> 
> So before reporting any defect: **sweep the budget or depth and see which way it moves.** It collapsing at
> higher effort means the engine was fine; it refusing to move means I am measuring my own arithmetic. Both
> answers are decided by one sweep that costs a few minutes, and both errors are avoided by it.

> **BEFORE CALLING SOMEONE'S NUMBER WRONG, RUN IT ON THEIR OWN SAMPLE.** A number can be **right on its own
> sample and wrong as a generalisation**, and those are different faults needing different reports. My G sample put
> 29-F45 **30 points** away from their published figures — far outside the ±5-point sampling error, and their own
> recipe invites a `REFUTES` line — so filing REFUTES would have been procedurally correct and **wrong**. Running
> their **own R_2911 set** reproduced all four numbers **to the digit** (87.5 / 87.5 / 85.0 / 92.5 %) and showed the
> difference was the **set**, not their measurement (07-C56). The correct report was "confirmed on its set, rate is
> set-dependent", which is more useful than either a bare CONFIRMS or a bare REFUTES. So: reproduce on the original
> sample first; only then decide whether you are correcting a measurement or bounding a generalisation.

> **WHEN MY SUMMARY LINE AND MY RAW DATA DISAGREE, BELIEVE THE DATA.** This caught **three** of my wrong
> verdicts tonight, and it is the cheapest self-check here: my script's printed `RESULT:` said "does not
> reproduce" three times while the per-row numbers in the same output already confirmed the claim. Each time
> the fault was a **wrong comparison**, not a wrong measurement — comparing python-chess's generation order
> against alphabetical order instead of the **engine's answer** against alphabetical order (07-C58); comparing
> `2|N|` against a positive mate's PV instead of `2N−1` (07-C51); calling a node inequality a regression without
> scoring the moves (07-C44). **Before reporting a verdict, read the rows, not the summary.** If they disagree,
> the summary is wrong, because a summary is a second implementation of the same logic and it gets less review.

> **A FABRICATED-MOVE CHECK NEEDS A POSITION WHERE THE ENGINE'S FIRST MOVE IS NOT ALPHABETICALLY FIRST.**
> On **startpos** the engine answers `a2a3` and the alphabetically first legal move is *also* `a2a3`, so a
> fabricated alphabetical move is **indistinguishable** from a real one. On **kiwipete** the engine answers
> `a2a3` while alphabetical is `a1b1`, so it **discriminates**. Use kiwipete (or any position like it) or the
> check can pass while measuring nothing (12-S-F30, confirmed in 07-C58).

> **A warning from a harness that has not validated its own input is not a finding.**
> Five of my controls were initially blind, and each was caught by its own planted fault, not by me.

> **For a side-to-move-relative score under a colour mirror, the invariant is EQUALITY, not
> negation.** `Board.mirror()` swaps colours *and* the side to move, so a +255 White advantage reads
> +255 for Black in the mirror. **The tell that catches a wrong checker: its "asymmetric" pairs share
> the same sign** (`orig cp −304` against `mirror cp −301`) — a negation-based checker also reports
> *fewer* symmetries, and its failures carry the **correct mirrored move**. Mine reported 4/120
> instead of 7/120 that way, and seat 16 had already documented the trap in its C5.

> **A damage table must exclude incomparable pairs.** Ranking positions by "score of the small budget's
> move minus score of the large budget's move" is only valid when both are centipawns. **Comparing a
> mate score against a centipawn loss is not a preference** — `mate −12` against `cp −2196` is two
> different losses, and subtracting them invents ~9,800 cp of damage. My run's largest gap, 95,212 cp,
> was exactly that artefact; it would have been the headline of any damage table I published (07-C44).

> **A small-sample rate does not order anything.** Two disjoint 100-position samples flipped the
> ordering of two node budgets ten points apart; over all 353 positions they were indistinguishable
> (07-C42/C43). Report a rate with its sample size, and say whether it ranks.

> **A node count under a clock stop is not reproducible until you prove it is.** Repeat the SAME cell
> on the SAME binary and see whether it reproduces before comparing anything across builds. Mine did
> not: at `go wtime 11 btime 11` one binary gave **412 nodes in one invocation and 971 in five others**,
> and 4 of 15 of my own sweep values failed their own repeats (07-C47). This applies to **every**
> node-count table taken under a wall-clock stop, **including my own earlier ones** — `job11`'s clock
> tables record a `nodes` column, and although I used it only as a parse check ("rows without nodes"),
> not as a cross-build comparison, any reader comparing those numbers should not. Under a clock, compare
> the **move** (discrete, reproducible) and the **wall time**, not the node count.

## How the negative in 07-R1 was made trustworthy (detector controls)

A "nothing happened" result is only worth something if the detector can fire. Two controls, both in this folder:

1. `job2/detector_control.cpp`, built with the same `-D_GLIBCXX_ASSERTIONS -D_GLIBCXX_DEBUG
   -fsanitize=address,undefined` flags as build-dbg, aborts with
   `Error: attempt to access an element in an empty container.` on `empty.front()`
   (exit 134). So the debug STL in this build really does catch empty `front()`.

   (`REACHED-LINE-2640 root_candidates=20` on `position startpos` + `go depth 2`),
   so the same instrumentation would have shown it being reached with an empty
   vector had that path existed.

-fsanitize=address,undefined`; `nm -C build-dbg/forge | grep -c __asan_` = 35.
