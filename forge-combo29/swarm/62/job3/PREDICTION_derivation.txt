ANALYTIC DERIVATION of the exposure condition.
Written before the measurement it predicts; kept as the prediction of record.

Notation: root halfmove clock H (98 or 99). Ply 0 = root. Assume no move by
either side in the tree resets the clock (no pawn move, no capture), so the node
at ply p carries clock H+p.

The engine's leaf rule (search.cpp:1302-1305) is

    if (hm >= 100) return !in_check() || has_legal_move();

i.e. the node at hm >= 100 is a DRAW unless that node is already checkmate.
The first such node is at ply

    j0 = 100 - H.

So the search reaches the true mate at ply p only if the mate node is reached
before any draw-pruned node: the checkmate has to BE the node at j0, because a
node at j0 that is not mate returns 0 and the subtree dies there.

  mate FOUND correctly (engine right)   <=>  p == j0
  mate MISSED   (engine returns 0)      <=>  p >  j0

Enumerating the two clocks against the smallest mates:

  H=99, j0=1: the mover at ply 1 is the LOSER (root side to move), who cannot
    mate. Any mate by the opponent lands at an even ply p >= 2 > j0. So a
    defender-side mate is ALWAYS missed at H=99. (A root-side mate in 1, p=1,
    is a checkmate AT j0 and is seen correctly -- that is the other branch of
    the rule and it works.)
  H=98, j0=2: a mate by the opponent in 1 move (p=2) lands exactly on j0 and is
    found. A mate in 2 moves (p=4) or more is missed.

PREDICTIONS THIS MAKES, each falsifiable:

  P1  At H=99, a position where the side to move is lost to a mate in 1 (which
      is the reported board) is scored 0. OBSERVED, matches.
  P2  The SAME board at H=98 is scored mate -1. OBSERVED, matches.
  P3  At H=98, a position where the side to move is lost to a mate in 2 is
      scored 0, even though a mate in 1 at H=98 is found. This is the parity
      split: the defect is not "halfmove 99" but "the mate does not land exactly
      on the clock-100 ply".
  P4  If a legal move by the side to move RESETS the clock (capture or pawn
      move), the clock never reaches 100 on that line and the engine must
      report the true value. This is the control that shows the rule only bites
      when the clock truly rolls over.
  P5  Positions that are GENUINELY drawn at hm >= 100 score 0, and that 0 is
      correct. Any checker that calls those bugs is broken; they are the
      control class the assignment asks for.

P3 is the prediction that decides whether this is a "one-ply-down" defect in
general or a narrow parity artefact. It is measured in job3/family_M2.
