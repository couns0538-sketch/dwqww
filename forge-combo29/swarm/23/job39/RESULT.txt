MATE CLAIMS VERIFIED BY REPLAY, NOT BY PV LENGTH
------------------------------------------------
A PV whose length equals 2N-1 is only internal consistency. `verify_mate.py` replays the PV in python-chess and
asserts the final position is checkmate, with a known-answer control that accepts fool's mate and REJECTS the same
line truncated by one move (both printed at the top of every run below):

  PC  counter 78, depth 12: PV is 12 plies, `mate 8` needs 15 → replay says **NO-MATE**, PV truncated.
  PC  counter 78, depth 16: PV is 15 plies → replay says **MATE**, d2a2 delivering mate.
  BASE counter 78, depth 16: PV is 17 plies, `mate 10` needs 19 → **NO-MATE**, truncated.
  BASE counter 78, depth 20: PV is 19 plies → replay says **MATE**, c5a5 delivering mate.

So both builds' mate SCORES are truthful at the depth where their PV completes, and both give a truncated PV at the
depth below it. That is a property of reporting a mate score with an incomplete PV, not of either build, and it is
worth knowing for any tool that trusts the PV: **a `mate N` score does not guarantee the PV in that same line is
long enough to deliver mate — at least one depth below the completed PV, it is not.** I checked the two cells I could
reach in the time; I did not sweep it.

LIMITS: one board throughout. Job 39 is base-vs-PC with NO PC20 switches; the switches change node counts (23-F82)
and were not measured here. The counter-89 PC cell at depths 18/20 is in flight. The "within one counter" tolerance in
job39's P4 was chosen before measuring, and the step did move by that one counter at depth 12 — P4 stands as written,
but the step is not identical, and the finding should say "within one counter" rather than "same".
