THE FINDING IS THE CONTRAST, which is why this was worth running:
- Drawn root (23-F60/61): the base's unsearched move loses **751-853 cp** — a minor piece — while the PC's holds.
- Lone-king boards: the base's move is worth between **-11 and +75 cp** of the PC's, and on three boards it is the
  SAME move. No material is lost and no board hides a blunder.

So the two defects have completely different cost profiles: the drawn-root defect makes the engine BLUNDER a piece
because it does not search at all, while the lone-king defect makes it FAIL TO CONVERT a won endgame — it plays a
reasonable move and simply does not find the mate at the searched depth. That distinction is the practically useful
conclusion: the drawn-root repair protects material, the lone-king repair improves conversion, and only the first is
a move-quality hazard.

P1 being missed is a small surprise worth stating: I expected the base and PC to agree on most boards because both
are searching, but they disagree on half of them (moves differ though the values are close) — which is consistent
with 23-F56's picture that a difference in depth, not a difference in understanding, is what changes the move.
