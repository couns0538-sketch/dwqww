PLAYING GENERATOR INDEX 0 COSTS A MEDIAN OF ~625 cp — and this job's own arithmetic needed THREE fixes
=========================================================================================================
23-F98/F100 established WHAT the 0-node move is (generator index 0). F60 and F96 measured what it cost on two boards.
This job measures the DISTRIBUTION, and it took three corrections to its own scoring before any number was usable.

THE RESULT, after the corrections
---------------------------------
Positions: every 400th line of the validated seed-2301 set (5000 positions), giving 12 evenly spread positions —
deterministic sampling, not hand-picked. Base build for g0 and the searched move, PC at depth 12 for scoring, mover's
view throughout.

**Costs in cp, excluding the one mate row: 40, 213, 442, 601, 607, 644, 1005, 1035, 1261, 2148, 2222 → median 625.5,
min 40, max 2,222. Ten of twelve are >= 300 cp.** Position 8 is reported separately because a mate is not a cp value:
on it the searched move mates in 5 and g0 does not, so g0 missed a forced mate outright — 1 of 12.

P1 **KILLED**: I predicted a median under 60 cp on the theory that index 0 is usually adequate. The measured median is
**625.5 cp**, an order of magnitude larger. The prediction's own kill condition was "median exactly 0", which the
weaker form of the prediction would have passed — but the substantive claim ("small but non-zero") is wrong and is
recorded as wrong.
P2 HELD: heavily skewed — median 625.5 with a tail to 2,222 cp plus a missed mate.
P3 HELD: g0 == the searched best move on **0 of 12**, so the comparison is between genuinely different moves.
P4 HELD after the fixes: the planted blunder (a move giving away a queen or rook) registers **+1,259 cp** of damage,
so the measure can see damage and the near-numbers are not blind.

THE THREE CORRECTIONS, in order, because the sequence is the lesson
-------------------------------------------------------------------
1. **A sign branch on the wrong side.** `sign = -1 if not b.turn else 1` branched on the side to move AFTER the move
   had been pushed — which is the OPPONENT — so every black-to-move row was inverted. Caught because the table said a
   depth-12 SEARCHED best move was 2,222 cp WORSE than an unsearched move, which is absurd on its face.
2. **A mate formula valid for one sign only.** `100000 - 1000*val` turns `mate -4` (the side to move IS mated) into
   **+104000**, i.e. a big win. Caught the second time the same position produced an absurd number.
3. **"Median" of an even-length list.** I printed `sorted[n//2]` (the upper of the two middle elements) and labelled it
   the median. The true median of eleven values is the sixth; my number was one rank too high. For twelve values the
   median is the mean of the 5th and 6th, so printing a single element needs the convention stated.

WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT MY OWN METHOD, plainly
--------------------------------------------
None of the three was caught by a control I designed. Each was caught because the NUMBER WAS ABSURD — a searched move
worse than an unsearched one, a mate scored as a win for the wrong colour, a median that disagreed with the sorted list
when I looked at it. A control that asserts "the searched best move must never be materially worse than generator
index 0" would have caught #1 and #2 immediately; that control did not exist and now should. **Absurdity is a detector
that depends on somebody rereading the table; an assertion does not.**

LIMITS: 12 positions from one seed set, `go depth 12` for scoring, one build pair, middlegame positions only (the drawn
roots behave differently by construction — there g0 is returned with 0 nodes and cp 0, so the "cost" there is measured
by F96's method, not this one). The PC is the scorer, so g0 and best are both judged by one engine's evaluation; the
planted case is what shows that judge can see real damage. The 625.5 cp median is a property of THESE 12 positions at
depth 12, and I do not extrapolate it to a rating loss without game results, which I have not measured.
