THE WITHIN-CLASS ORDER IS ASCENDING FROM-SQUARE — a refinement, and a correction of my own earlier reading
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Job 51 concluded the within-class order was "clearly not by from-square", because python-chess's `legal_moves` iterates
pairs in DESCENDING file order, so the pawn list I compared against (`h2h3, g2g3, f2f3`) was the reverse of the
engine's. The engine's actual divide order is:

| case | engine's pawn-class members in its own order | engine move |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | f2f3, g2g3, h2h3 | f2f3 |
| P2 | f7f6, g7g6, h7h6 | f7f6 |
| P5 | f2f3, g2g3, h2h3 | f2f3 |

**Ascending from-square** (f2 = 13, g2 = 14, h2 = 15). So the full rule is:

  **on a drawn root the engine plays the lowest-from-square single pawn push it generates** — and if there are no pawn
  moves, the same class ordering continues into knight, bishop, rook, queen, king.

That is a completely specified, position-independent prediction for these positions.

**A FALSE ALARM AND ITS RESOLUTION — the full sequence, because the sequence is the point.**

My first draft of the paragraph above claimed the from-square rule "also explains 23-F100's e6e5 (from-square 44 = the
lowest black pawn)". I checked that and it looked FALSE: on that board black's pawns sit on d5=35, e6=44, h6=47, a7=48,
b7=49, f7=53, g7=54, so d5 (35) is lower than e6 (44) and I wrote a correction saying the from-square rule was
CONTRADICTED.

**That correction was itself wrong.** The d5 pawn is BLOCKED: rank 4 of the FEN is `N2P1B1P`, i.e. a WHITE PAWN on d4,
so `d5d4` is not a legal move and never appears in any divide. python-chess confirms `d5d4 in legal_moves == False`. The
pawn moves black actually has are `e6e5` (44), `h6h5` (47), `a7a6`/`a7a5` (48) — and the lowest from-square among
GENERATED pawn pushes is 44 = **e6e5**. So the ascending-from-square rule is **not contradicted by 23-F100**; it holds
there too.

The lesson is about what I compared against: I ranked pawns by their SQUARE without asking whether each pawn's push was
GENERATED. A rule about the order of generated moves must be checked against the generated set, not against all pieces
of the type on the board. This is the third time in this session that a comparison operand rather than the hypothesis was
at fault (23-F104 x2), and the pattern is consistent: I get the measurement right and the yardstick wrong.

What the job-52 result establishes: on five drawn roots, an independently computed class-based predictor named the
engine's 0-node move 5 of 5, and the within-class order was ascending from-square in every case where it applied; a

What the job-52 result DOES establish is narrower and still useful: on five drawn roots, an independently computed
