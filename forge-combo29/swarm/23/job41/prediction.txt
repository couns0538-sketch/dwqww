Depths 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 on both builds, Threads 1, Hash 16, OwnBook false, no PC20 switches. For every cell

replay ends in MATE.

Prediction (each with the range that kills it):
  P1  THE WINDOW IS NARROW: on each board/build, at most TWO depths report `mate N` with a too-short PV before a depth
      reports it with a mating PV.                                         KILLED if any board/build shows three or more.
  P2  IT DOES NOT PERSIST AT DEPTH: at depth 18 every `mate N` cell replays to MATE on every board/build. If a build
      is still truncating at 18, that is the stronger hazard and the finding must say so.
                                                                           KILLED if any depth-18 mate cell fails replay.
  P3  THE TOO-SHORT PV IS ALWAYS EXACTLY ONE PLY SHORT IN ITS REPORTED DISTANCE — i.e. PV plies = 2N-3, not 2N-5 or
      shorter (the score is one ply optimistic, not several).             KILLED if any short cell is >= 2 plies short
      by the 2N-1 measure... with the caveat that a PV can also be truncated for display reasons unrelated to the
      distance, which is what P3 is testing for.
  P4  A CONTROL ON THE METHOD ITSELF: the same sweep on a DRAWN board (`8/8/8/8/8/8/8/K6k w`, all moves cp 0) reports
      NO mate cell at any depth on either build — so any mate cell in this job is a property of a won board, not of
      the harness's parsing.                                               KILLED if a mate appears on the drawn board.

If P1-P4 hold: F86 becomes "a mate score is one ply optimistic about its own PV for at most two depths, and never at
depth 18", which is a bounded, testable hazard a GUI author can act on (replay the PV; do not trust its length).
If P1 or P2 fails, the hazard is wider than F86's two cells suggested and F86 must be upgraded, not just extended.
