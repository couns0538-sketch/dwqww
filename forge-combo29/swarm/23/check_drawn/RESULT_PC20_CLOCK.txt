ANSWER: the six boards reach depths **22, 22, 23, 20, 19 and 17** — so **four of six reach the depth-20 threshold and
two do not**. Board 5 lands at 19 (one short) yet still reports `mate 9`, so the threshold is not a hard cliff: it
varies by position, and at 19 that board already finds its mate. Board 6 reaches only 17 and reports cp.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR MATCH PLAY, which is the point of the table:
- The repair DOES fire in real play on the four boards that reach depth 20+ (and 5 already mates at 19), so it is
  not academic.
- On board 6 the clock reaches depth 17, below where the mate surfaces, so **the repair never fires there in a
  6 s+0.06 game** — the PC behaves like the base on that board at this clock.
- The depth-20 "threshold" is therefore a rough description, not a switch: what matters is whether the clock's
  depth on THAT board passes the point where its mate becomes visible, and that point differs per board.

base and the PC are closer in real play than the depth-24 comparison suggested. My earlier "no benefit below depth
