ALL FOUR PREDICTIONS HELD, and the result is stronger than predicted:
- P1: five qualifying games (needed >= 2).
- P2: the base answered **0 nodes in all five**.
- P3: the base's move was worth **less than 0 cp in all five** (I predicted "at least half").
- P4: the PC searched in all five (532,851 to 1,602,068 nodes).

THE WORST CASE, which is the headline: in game 2 the base's unsearched move is **`mate -5`** — it walks into a forced
mate in 5 plies — while the PC's move holds at cp 0. In game 3 the unsearched move is **cp -1858**, close to a lost
position. So on genuine repetitions the defect does not merely pick a different move: it can lose the game outright,
and the score it reports (cp 0) hides that completely.

So the corrected statement, replacing 23-F70's "general but not universal": **on every strictly-qualifying repetition
tested — five of five — the base answers without searching and plays a worse move, from -31 cp to a forced mate
against it, while the PC searches and holds.** That is a defect with real cost on a position normal self-play reaches.

LIMITS: five games, one settings combination, all from self-play of this build against itself in this session; one of
