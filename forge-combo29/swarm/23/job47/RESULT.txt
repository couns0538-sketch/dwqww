A NOTE ON WHAT THIS JOB WAS WORTH, since it produced a negative
---------------------------------------------------------------
It took twelve minutes and it converted "measured on one fixture, flagged as such" into "tested for reproduction on
three, found on one, and here is exactly why the other two could not answer". That is a different epistemic state from
either an unqualified general claim or an untested caveat, and it cost one prediction file to get.

INSTANCE 20, and it is a NEW shape: my P2 was written as "at least two of three roots", which SILENTLY ASSUMED all
three roots would yield valid fixtures and that all three would have a mate available. Both assumptions were about my
own fixtures, not about the engine — I predicted the engine's behaviour while leaving my fixtures' viability
unpredicted. The rule it earns: **when a prediction is phrased over N fixtures, the number of fixtures that will
actually be measurable is itself a claim, and it needs checking before the prediction means anything.** Print the
eligible count, then evaluate the prediction against it.
