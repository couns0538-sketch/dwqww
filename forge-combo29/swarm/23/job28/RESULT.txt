DOES THE RECOMMENDED PROTOCOL PREVENT THE HAZARDS? — both, with controls that prove it
==================================================================================
Two drivers, identical except the `info string` check. Each plays game 1 (so a stale board of 42 legal moves exists),
is given a hazard input, then asked for a move. Intended boards were chosen (and PRINTED, with their disjointness
verified) so the returned move reveals which board was searched.

| driver | scenario | bestmove | legal on INTENDED? | legal on STALE? | board searched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NAIVE** | H1: refused FEN for the new game | **g1f3** | **no** | **yes** | **STALE — wrong board** |
| COMPLIANT | H1: refused FEN for the new game | f7g7 | yes | no | INTENDED — correct |
| **NAIVE** | H2: illegal token MID-LIST (`f2f3 f7f6` + illegal `f2f3` + rest) | **e2e4** | **no** | **no** | **neither — the truncated prefix** |
| COMPLIANT | H2: same input | d7d5 | yes | no | INTENDED — correct |

Both hazards now discriminate, and the controls carry the result:
- **H1**: the naive driver answers a move ILLEGAL on the board it meant to play (stale board searched); the compliant
  one answers `f7g7`, legal on the intended board and illegal on the stale one.
- **H2**: the naive driver answers `e2e4`, legal on **neither** board — because a mid-list illegal token truncates the
  game to a 2-ply prefix, so it searched a THIRD board that is neither the stale one nor the intended one. That is
  worse than H1 and is exactly the "silently playing a different game" shape. The compliant driver rebuilds and
  answers `d7d5`, legal on the intended board.

WHY THE CONSTRUCTION MATTERED, twice: my first H2 attempt appended the illegal token at the END, so the legal prefix
EQUALLED the intended board and the naive driver looked correct; and my first H1 attempt compared against the
truncated board rather than the intended one, so both drivers looked correct. Both errors were caught by checking
that the boards' legal sets were actually disjoint before believing the result.

PREDICTIONS: P1 held (compliant plays 0 wrong moves in both scenarios); P2 held (it rebuilt on both); P3 held (the
naive driver failed BOTH, not just one); P4 held (no hangs).

HAZARD 3 VALIDATED TOO — both clocks answers, one clock never does
==================================================================
Black to move (so the clock that matters is `btime`), same build/settings, bounded 3 s wait:

| driver | `go` sent | outcome | diagnostics |
|---|---|---|---|
| NAIVE | `go wtime 1000` | **NO BESTMOVE in 3 s** | 0 |
| COMPLIANT | `go wtime 1000 btime 1000 winc 10 binc 10` | **answered 0.03 s (e7e5)** | 0 |

So the third rule in the driver block is validated the same way as the other two: the one-clock `go` never answers and
says nothing, the both-clocks `go` answers in 30 ms. The control is the compliant row — without it, "no bestmove"
could have meant a broken harness.

ALL THREE HAZARDS ARE NOW VALIDATED AGAINST THE RECOMMENDED DRIVER, each with a control that shows what the naive
driver does instead:
  H1 refused FEN   -> naive searches the STALE board; compliant rebuilds and plays on the intended board
  H2 illegal token -> naive searches a THIRD board (the truncated prefix); compliant rebuilds
  H3 partial clock -> naive never gets a move; compliant sends both clocks and gets one in 30 ms
