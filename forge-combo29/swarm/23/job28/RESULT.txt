DOES THE RECOMMENDED PROTOCOL PREVENT THE HAZARDS? — for H1, yes, and the naive driver provably fails
===============================================================================================
Two drivers, identical except the `info string` check. Each plays game 1 (so a stale board exists), is then given a
hazard input, and asked for a move. The INTENDED boards were chosen so their legal sets are DISJOINT from the stale
board's — 26 and 27 moves, overlap ZERO with the stale board's 42 — so the returned move reveals WHICH board was
actually searched.

| driver | scenario | bestmove | legal on INTENDED? | legal on STALE? | board searched |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **NAIVE** | H1: refused FEN for the new game | **g1f3** | **no** | **yes** | **STALE — a wrong board** |
| COMPLIANT | H1: refused FEN for the new game | f7g7 | yes | no | **INTENDED — correct** |
| NAIVE | H2: illegal token in the move list | g1f3 | yes | yes | ambiguous |
| COMPLIANT | H2: illegal token in the move list | g1f3 | yes | yes | ambiguous |

THE ANSWER: for H1 the protocol works and the control proves it — the naive driver answers a move that is **illegal
on the board it meant to play** (stale board searched), while the compliant driver, having counted the invalid-FEN
line and rebuilt, answers `f7g7`, legal on the intended board and illegal on the stale one. That is a clean
discrimination, and it is exactly the pair of facts that makes the result meaningful.

H2 REMAINS AMBIGUOUS on this construction, and I say so rather than scoring it: the two-ply board after `e2e4 e7e5`
shares its entire legal-move set with the stale 42-move board, so `g1f3` is legal on both and cannot say which was
searched. Answering H2 properly needs a stale board and an intended board whose sets are disjoint AND reachable from
`startpos`, which I did not build.

PREDICTION SCORED: P1 (compliant plays 0 wrong moves) held on H1 and untested on H2. P2 held for H1 (it rebuilt). P3
held for H1 — the naive driver played a move illegal on its intended board, so the control shows the protocol is
what prevents the error. P4 held (no hangs).
