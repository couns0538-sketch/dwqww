CHECK of seat 11's S-F1 — junk after the sixth FEN field is silently ignored
=========================================================================
Claim: `position fen` reads only six tokens and the following loop discards any extra token, so a malformed FEN with
junk after the sixth field is ACCEPTED rather than refused.

Method (mine): send startpos first, then the test FEN, then `go perft 1`. startpos answers perft 1 = 20, so 20 means
"refused and startpos kept"; any other count means the test board was taken. Base build, Threads 1, Hash 16.

| case | invalid-FEN line? | perft 1 | reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| clean startpos-like | no | 20 | accepted, and 20 is startpos's own count |
| junk word after 6th | no | 20 | ACCEPTED — no refusal, board is startpos as asked |
| junk number after 6th | no | 20 | ACCEPTED |
| junk gibberish `@@@ $$$` | no | 20 | ACCEPTED |
| junk on a MID-GAME FEN | no | **27** | **ACCEPTED** — 27 is that mid-game board's own count |
| mid-game clean twin | no | **27** | identical to the junk version |
| CONTROL: too many squares | yes — `invalid FEN: invalid placement` | 20 | REFUSED, startpos kept |

VERDICT: CONFIRMS S-F1. The decisive row is the mid-game pair: the junk-suffixed FEN gives exactly the clean
twin's count (27), so the junk was ignored and the position used — not refused. The control proves the same harness
CAN see a refusal (the too-many-squares FEN is refused and leaves startpos at 20), so the "no invalid-FEN line" in
the other rows is a real absence, not a blind checker.

CONSEQUENCE worth stating: a GUI or test harness that builds a FEN by concatenation and ends up with a trailing
field (a stray space plus a token, a seventh field from a buggy formatter) gets its position searched silently and
correctly-with-respect-to-the-first-six-fields. That is forgiving rather than dangerous, but it means "the engine
accepted my FEN" does not imply "my FEN was valid".
