CHECK of seat 20's S-F27 — the tie-break cannot be read from the UCI protocol
============================================================================
Claims: on a lone-king position with 3 legal moves, all score cp 0, the engine returns a1b1 at depths 1-3 while
python-chess's FIRST legal move is a1b2, so `bestmove` does not reveal which move came first in the engine's list.

My position (built from their description): `8/8/8/8/8/8/8/K6k w - - 0 1` — white Ka1, black Kh8.
python-chess's legal moves in ITS order: a1b2, a1a2, a1b1  (first = a1b2)

| depth | score | bestmove | is it python-chess's first? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | cp 0 | a1b1 | NO |
| 2 | cp 0 | a1b1 | NO |
| 3 | cp 0 | a1b1 | NO |

Then I tested the part that decides whether other seats can do tie-break work at all: each move searched ALONE with
`go depth 3 searchmoves <move>`:
| move | score alone |
|---|---|
| a1b2 | cp 0 |
| a1a2 | cp 0 |
| a1b1 | cp 0 |
All three tie at cp 0, so no probing of individual moves recovers an ORDER — only the scores.

VERDICT: CONFIRMS S-F27. The engine returns a move that is NOT python-chess's first legal move, all three score cp 0,
and no combination of `searchmoves` probing exposes which move the engine considered first. Any tie-break claim
based on `bestmove` on such a position is unfounded, and their conclusion that it must come from the source or a
logging build is right.

A LIMIT OF MY CHECK, stated: I intended to test their source note about a 4+ vs <4 move branch by using a position
with a fourth legal square, but the position I chose (`8/8/8/8/8/8/8/K3k3 w`) also has exactly 3 legal moves, so
that part of their finding is NOT tested here. Testing it needs a constructed position with 4 or 5 legal moves that
all score identically, which I did not build.
