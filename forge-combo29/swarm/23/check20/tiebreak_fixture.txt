FIXTURE AUDIT of my own tie-break test (job 28's lesson applied to 23-C17)
=========================================================================
23-F67 taught that a fixture is part of the control: if the correct and the broken behaviour produce the SAME
observable output, the fixture cannot distinguish and the result is worthless.

Applying that to my tie-break check (23-C17, the 3-vs-5 legal-move pair):
- The claim was "bestmove != first legal move, and the two widths behave alike".
- Could a BROKEN engine produce the same output? Yes trivially: any engine returning SOME legal move that is not
  python-chess's first would match. So the test as written CANNOT distinguish "the engine has its own ordering" from
  "the engine returns an arbitrary legal move".
- What it DOES establish, and all I claimed: bestmove is not the first move in python-chess's order, on both widths,
  deterministically. That is a statement about the PROTOCOL's limits, not about the engine's internal order.

So the result stands as I worded it, and the limit I wrote there ("python-chess's order is a reference, not the
engine's order") is the same point made independently. Recording it here so the fixture question is answered
explicitly rather than left implicit.
