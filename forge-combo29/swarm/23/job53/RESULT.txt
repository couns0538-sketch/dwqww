THE PREDICTOR HOLDS ON A NATURAL ROOT — ONCE THE ROOT IS SENT THE WAY THE PATH REQUIRES, and my first run tested the wrong thing
=================================================================================================================================
Two results, and the second one is the important correction.

WHAT THE FIRST RUN FOUND, AND WHY IT WAS A TEST OF THE WRONG THING
------------------------------------------------------------------
Job 53's stage 4 fed each "natural root" to the base as a BARE FEN. The naturally reached threefold root from the 1.e4
self-play game (`1k6/3q1p1p/rp5p/4P3/pQ3P2/7P3P...  b - - 15 40`, threefold at ply 78) then came back **searched, 44,888
nodes, bestmove `d7c8`** — nothing like the constructed roots, which bail at 0 nodes. That looked like a refutation of
the whole family.

It was not. A bare FEN carries no move history, so the engine cannot see the repetition. The SAME position, sent the way
the drawn-root path actually requires — reached by a move list — behaves exactly as every constructed fixture:

| how the position was sent | score | nodes | bestmove |
|---|---|---|---|
| bare FEN (no history) | cp -106 | **44,888** | d7c8 |
| reached by an 8-ply cycle `b8c8 b4e7 c8b8 e7b4` ×2 | **cp 0** | **0** | **a4a3** |

So the drawn-root mechanism is triggered by HISTORY, not by the position. My stage-4 harness reproduced the very mistake
the whole family is about, one level up: I sent a position without its history and measured what the engine did with a
position it could not know was repeated. The lesson is not new to me (23-F76 established that the drawn-root case
requires a MOVE LIST, no FEN involved) — and I still made the mistake, in a job whose purpose was to test that family.

THE CORRECTED RESULT on the natural root
-----------------------------------------
Sent by move list, the natural threefold root gives **cp 0, 0 nodes, bestmove `a4a3`**.
The predictor, computed independently in python-chess before the engine was asked, predicted class `pawn-single` and the
move **`a4a3`**. The engine's move is **exactly the predicted move**.

So 23-F105's predictor holds on a root I did not construct, chosen by self-play, which is the stronger claim F105 could
not make on its own. That is **1 natural root**, not five, and I will not present it as more than one instance.

P-verdicts for job 53 as written
---------------------------------
P1 KILLED AS WRITTEN, and the kill was my harness's fault twice over: (a) my stage-1 loop counted occurrences only up to
the second-to-last position and missed that the game's FINAL position was the threefold; (b) my first follow-up then
labelled that final position "other draw" by INFERENCE rather than by measuring the flags — I guessed CHECKMATE and was
wrong. The flags, measured: 1.e4 → **THREEFOLD at ply 78**; 1.d4 → **CHECKMATE at ply 136**; 1.Nf3 → no end condition
within 150 plies. So exactly one natural threefold root exists in these three games.
P2 (0 nodes on every natural root) HOLDS once the root is sent with its history: 0 nodes. It appeared to fail only
because the bare-FEN send is not the drawn-root path.
P3 (predictor names the move) HOLDS on the one natural root, exactly, at the within-class level too.
P4 (at least one pawn-single root) HOLDS: the natural root's class is pawn-single.

WHAT THIS ADDS, stated narrowly
--------------------------------
The predictor is not a property of fixtures I built: on one root reached by the engine's own play, the independently
predicted move is the move played, with 0 nodes and cp 0. The mechanism is history-gated, which is why the bare-FEN
control searched 44,888 nodes — and that contrast is itself a clean demonstration of the gate.

INSTANCE 23 OF THE FAMILY, and the most embarrassing one because I already knew the rule: **I built a harness that sent a
drawn root as a bare FEN, in a job whose subject was the drawn-root family that is DEFINED by requiring a move list.**
The failure was silent (44,888 nodes and a plausible move), and I initially read it as a refutation of my own finding.
The check that caught it was asking "what does this same position do when sent WITH its history" — i.e. varying the one
thing the mechanism depends on. **When a mechanism is history-dependent, a harness that drops history is not a weaker
test, it is a test of something else — and it will produce a confident, plausible, wrong answer.**

LIMITS: one natural root, one self-play game, base build, `go depth 10`. The 1.d4 game ended in checkmate and the 1.Nf3
game did not end, so the "natural" evidence is one instance; generating more would need games designed to repeat, at
which point they would stop being naturally reached.
