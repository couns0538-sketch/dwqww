ALL FOUR PREDICTIONS HELD, each against its stated kill range:
  P1 eval constant at 406 across all fourteen cells, spread 0 cp at BOTH depths (kill was > 5). Holds.
  P2 depth 6: onset 95, inside the predicted 93-97, and go is a WIN at both 80 (cp 400) and 90 (cp 401) — so the

  P3 depth 12: onset 89, inside 87-91, and go is a WIN at 78 (cp 410) and 84 (cp 364). Holds.
  P4 the onsets are 95 and 89 — SIX counters apart, where the kill was "within 3". Holds.

WHY THIS IS BETTER THAN A REPEAT: the onsets land EXACTLY on 23-C26's independently measured values for the same
depths on the same board — C26 got 95 at depth 6 and 89 at depth 12 by sweeping the counter and reading the search's
own score; this got 95 and 89 by watching the disagreement between a static evaluator and a searched answer. Two
instruments, no shared code, exact agreement at two depths. F80 could previously claim that agreement at ONE depth
(92 vs 91, within ±4); it now holds exactly at two, and the depth-dependence that F80 only cited is measured here.

ONE REFINEMENT F80 DID NOT HAVE, and it cuts against the shorthand: BELOW the horizon far from it, the gap is not
reliably positive — depth 12 counter 78 gives gap **-4** (eval 406, go cp 410). So "eval overstates by 406" is true
only at and past the horizon; below it the two interfaces essentially agree, with the search sometimes slightly
ABOVE the static number. The right sentence for a tool author is "the two interfaces agree below the horizon and
diverge by the whole eval total at it", not "eval overstates".

THE ONSET REGION IS ALSO WHERE THE COST EXPLODES: 11.9M nodes at counter 88, 176M at 89, 34M at 90 (depth 12).
Deliberately NOT used as an argument about performance — per 23-F82, a node count is not a defect and this is one
cell on one board. It is recorded because a tool that searches across the horizon should not be surprised by the
cost, and because it is the only place in this job where the node count is not small.

LIMIT: one board, two depths, base build only. The step was not measured on the PC r15 build, so nothing here says
the repair moves the step (23-C26 measured the PC's behaviour at counters 0-91, not at the onset). Onset defined as
the first counter at which go is cp 0 AND every higher counter tested is also cp 0 — depth 6 counter 93 (cp 327) is
below 94 (cp 392), a non-monotonic wobble recorded rather than smoothed, and the highest counter tested per depth
(6: 98; 12: 90) is cp 0 so the permanence claim is not contradicted within the tested range, but neither is it
extended beyond it.
