THE BASE'S 0-NODE MOVE STILL WINS — BUT IT THROWS AWAY THE MATE, and that is a different defect from 23-F60's
=================================================================================================================
23-F93 established that the base returns cp 0 with 0 nodes on a threefold root on either side. Job 46 asks the question
that decides what F93 is worth: does the unsearched move COST anything?

METHOD: take each build's chosen move on the fixture W-3 (`1K6/8/8/3R4/8/8/8/k7 w` moves
`d5d6 a1b1 d6d5 b1a1 d5d6 a1b1 d6d5 b1a1`, root occurs 3×, white to move, winning). Pushed with python-chess so the
resulting board is independently established, then scored by the PC at depth 16.

STEP 1 — WHAT EACH BUILD CHOOSES ON THE THREEFOLD ROOT
| build | score | nodes | bestmove |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | cp 0 | **0** | **d5d1** (rook to the first rank, next to black's king on a1) |
| PC r15 | cp 362 | 56,153 | d5b5 |

Both moves legal per python-chess. CONTROL: the root itself is winning for white — the PC scores it **mate 10** at
depth 16 (99,831 nodes) — so "the move still wins" cannot be an artefact of a board that was drawn anyway.

STEP 2 — THE POSITION AFTER EACH MOVE, PC at depth 16
| after | board | PC score | nodes |
|---|---|---|---|
| base's **d5d1** | `1K6/8/8/8/8/8/8/k2R4 b - - 9 5` | **cp -529** | 311,633 |
| PC's **d5b5** | `1K6/8/8/1R6/8/8/8/k7 b - - 9 5` | **mate -9** | 35,923 |

P1 HELD, on its stated test: after BOTH moves white is still winning (cp -529 and mate -9 are both from black's point
of view). So the base's unsearched move does **not** throw the game away, and this fixture is a WEAKER case than
23-F60 — where the 0-node move lost outright. Stated plainly because it would be easy to let F60's stronger result
be borrowed by this one.

BUT P1's letter understates what happened, and the honest finding is the second half:
**THE BASE'S MOVE DESTROYS THE MATE SCORE AND NEVER RECOVERS IT.** On the root the PC finds mate 10 (99,831 nodes).
After d5d1 that becomes a capped +529, and deepening does not restore it:

| depth after d5d1 | score | nodes |
|---|---|---|
| 16 | cp -529 | 311,633 |
| 18 | cp -533 | 388,563 |
| 20 | cp -533 | 490,580 |

Stable across 16/18/20. The board is KRvK with black holding only a king and just two legal replies (a1b2, a1a2), so
the win is a textbook one and the engine can SEE it is winning; it simply cannot find a mate inside its horizon from
that rook placement, because driving the king from the a-file corner it placed itself beside is a long manoeuvre. The
PC's d5b5 keeps the mate at 9. So:

  P2's letter ("the two scores differ by less than a mate") is **KILLED** — the difference IS a mate, and that is the
  interesting part: the base's move converts a 10-move mate into an indefinitely long win.
  P3 held: the base reports a draw on a position it wins, plays an unsearched move, and that move preserves the RESULT
  while degrading the METHOD.

WHAT F93 IS WORTH, stated without inflation
--------------------------------------------
Two distinct costs, and only the first is measurable here:
1. **Reporting cost (measured, serious for a tool):** the base says cp 0 on a position that is mate in 10. A GUI
   showing "0.00" beside a winning position has been told something false, and no diagnostic accompanies it.
2. **Playing cost (measured on this fixture, modest; UNMEASURED in general):** the move chosen without a search is
   legal and still winning, but it discards a mate in 9 for a long grind. On a board where an unsearched move can LOSE,
   the cost would be the result — 23-F60 shows that case exists — but this job does not measure it and I will not
   generalise from one endgame fixture to that.

LIMITS: one board, one fixture, K+R v K, `go depth` only, PC used as the reference scorer (so both "still winning"
verdicts share one engine as their source — the material count backs them independently: black has only a king, so
white wins by force). The mate-10-on-the-root control is what prevents the whole table from being about a drawn board.
