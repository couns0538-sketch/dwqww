THREE BUDGETS ON FRESH POSITIONS — is seat 17's 1-in-10 a rate or a board?
=======================================================================
Setup: PC r15 build, PC20, Threads 1, Hash 16, a fresh process per (position, budget), stdout drained to
bestmove (19-F12). 20 positions from my SEED-2401 games, chosen quiet (no check, no capture available, >= 20
legal moves), never searched before by seat 17 or by my job 7. Budgets 20000 / 50000 / 250000 as file 6 gives
the PC's match path. 5 fresh runs per cell = 300 searches.

RESULT: **6 of 20 fresh positions change their move between the match path's three budgets** — three times the
rate seat 17 and I both saw on set-G positions (1 of 10). So 1-in-10 was a property of those board types, not a
rate: on quiet middlegames chosen to have several plausible moves the effect is common.

SHAPES (this is where my prediction was wrong): only 4 of the 6 behave as I predicted ("20000 is the weak one,
50000 and 250000 agree"). The exceptions are real and matter:
  - `rnbqkb1r/1p2nppp/...` changes at ALL THREE budgets: h4g2 -> f1g2 -> d2d3.
  - `3n3r/4kn2/...` keeps f7g5 at 20000 and 50000 but switches to d8c6 at 250000, i.e. MORE budget changes the
    move, which is the opposite of "the small budget is the weak one".
So "the 20000 budget is the weak player" is not a safe summary: raising the budget can change the move in either
direction.

DETERMINISM: 60 of 60 (position, budget) cells gave identical bestmove AND identical node count across 5 fresh
processes. Nothing here is noise, and the PC20 regime has no drift (P5 also held).

LEGALITY: 300 of 300 bestmoves legal (python-chess), with a planted control confirming the checker can fire
(e2e5 is rejected on startpos).

LARGER SAMPLE — 60 fresh quiet positions, 900 searches, same protocol
====================================================================
| measure | value |
|---|---|
| positions where the move changes between the three budgets | **18 of 60 (30 %)** |
| 20000 differs, 50000 == 250000 | 4 |
| 250000 differs, 20000 == 50000 | 11 |
| all three differ | 3 |
| non-deterministic (position, budget) cells | 0 of 180 |

So on FRESH quiet positions the effect is common: 30 %, not the 10 % seat 17 and I saw on set-G boards.
And the dominant shape is the OPPOSITE of the intuitive one: the single most common pattern (11 of 18) is that
20000 and 50000 agree and only 250000 changes the move. "The 20000 budget is the weak player" would be the wrong
summary — the larger budget changes the move more often.

Combined with the 20-position run (6 of 20 = 30 %, same rate), this is a consistent 30 % on quiet middlegames.

A CAUSE I TESTED AND REJECTED (recorded so nobody repeats it)
============================================================
First hypothesis: the move changes because the 250000 budget simply reaches a deeper ply and sees more. That is
TRUE but it does not explain the change, because the depth gain is the same on the positions that do NOT change:

| group | depth(250000) - depth(20000): min / median / max |
|---|---|
| positions whose move CHANGED (18) | 3 / 5.0 / 7 |
| positions whose move did NOT change (42) | 3 / 6.0 / 9 |

So all 60 positions gain 3-9 plies at the largest budget and only 18 change their move. "It searches deeper" is
therefore not a distinguishing cause; the difference must be in how close the top moves are evaluated, which I
have NOT measured. This stays "suspicion, not proven" and is reported as such.
