THE BAIL IS STRICTLY ROOT-ONLY — a threefold in the history does NOT stop the search
=====================================================================================
Every fixture in this family put the threefold AT THE ROOT, which is not how a real game looks: the repetition usually
happens a few plies earlier and play continues. This tests whether the engine bails when the drawn position is behind it
rather than in front of it. I had no prior expectation, and the answer narrows the family rather than widening it.

FIXTURES on `1K6/8/8/3R4/8/8/8/k7 w`, cycle `b8c8 a1b2 c8b8 b2a1`, each checked with `job59/validate.py`

| case | move list | root occurrences | nodes | score | move | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S-A threefold root | cycle ×2 | **3** | **0** | cp 0 | d5d1 | **BAIL** |
| **S-B threefold + 1 ply** | cycle ×2 then `d5d6` | **3** (in history) | **12,978** | cp -417 | a1b2 | **searched** |
| S-C control, two occurrences + 1 ply | cycle ×1 then `d5d6` | 2 | 8,734 | cp -414 | a1b2 | searched |

P1 HELD: S-A bails, reproducing 23-F125 on this board.
**P2 KILLED, and that is the informative outcome: S-B SEARCHES.** With a genuine threefold three plies behind the searched
position, the engine does a full 12,978-node search and reports cp -417 — it does not treat the game as drawn.
P3 HELD: the two-occurrence control searches (8,734 nodes).

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR ANYONE USING THE ENGINE
--------------------------------------------
**CORRECTED AND SHARPENED BY THE LONGER-CONTINUATION TEST BELOW — the bail is about the SEARCHED POSITION's own count,
wherever that position sits in the move list, not about the game's initial position. My first wording here said
"strictly root-only", which was imprecise: a 3-ply continuation bails (see the table at the end) because the position AT
THE END OF THE MOVE LIST has itself occurred three times.** A tool
that has played into a repetition and then asks the engine about the NEXT position gets an ordinary search. Combined with
23-F109 (a bare FEN searches, because the history is absent), the full condition is:

> the bail requires the ROOT's FIDE identity to have occurred three times **in the move list sent with it**.

That is the precise, complete statement — and it means the hazard is NARROWER than the family's earlier framing
suggested in one direction and WIDER than "a bare FEN" in another: a tool that sends move lists is exposed only on the
exact repeated position, and one that sends FENs is never exposed at all, while a tool that plays through a repetition
and keeps searching gets normal behaviour.

WHY THE SEARCHED-BUT-DRAWN CASE IS STILL A TRAP, though a different one
------------------------------------------------------------------------
S-B searched and returned cp -417 — a losing score for the side to move — on a position three plies after a threefold.
The game was already drawn by repetition before that move was made, so the score is about a position that cannot legally
arise in a real game that ended at the repetition. That is not the bail defect; it is the ordinary consequence of asking
about a position past the end of a drawn game, and no engine can be expected to know the game ended three plies ago
unless it is told. **Worth saying plainly so nobody re-reads this as a new defect: the searched cell is correct
behaviour on a nonsensical question.**

LIMITS: one board, `go depth 10`, one ply past the repetition; a longer continuation (5 or 10 plies past) is untested and
would presumably behave the same way, since the mechanism is the same.

THE LONGER-CONTINUATION TEST, which corrected my own wording
============================================================
I had written "a longer continuation would presumably behave the same way". It does not, and the reason refines 23-F125.
Continuing past the threefold by N plies (`d5d6 a1b2 d6d5 b2a1 d5d7 a1b2 d7d5 b2a1 d5b5 a1b2`), every line checked legal
and every identity computed with the corrected key:

| plies past the repetition | root occurrences | **SEARCHED-position occurrences** | nodes | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | **1** | 12,978 | searched |
| **3** | 3 | **3** | **0** | **BAIL** |
| 5 | 4 | **1** | 11,691 | searched |

**The verdict tracks the SEARCHED POSITION's own occurrence count, exactly: 1 → searches, 3 → bails, 1 → searches.**
Not the root's count, which is 3 in all three cases.

So 23-F125's statement needs restating in the form the data actually supports:

> the base returns 0 nodes **iff the position being searched** — the position at the end of the sent move list — has
> occurred at least three times in that list.

This is a REFINEMENT, not a refutation: every fixture in 23-F125/F132/F133 had the repeated position at the end of the
list (which is also the "root" when the list is empty or ends there), so those results stand unchanged. What changes is
the SCOPE: a tool that sends a long move list can have the engine bail at the END of that list if the final position
repeats three times, and this is not about the game's starting position at all.

And it also explains the k=1 and k=5 cells without a second mechanism: after an odd number of further plies the searched
position is a FRESH one that has occurred once, so the engine searches normally — which is correct behaviour.

LIMITS: one board, `go depth 10`, three continuation lengths; the k=10 line I first wrote contained an illegal move and
was EXCLUDED rather than patched, so the longest tested continuation is 5 plies.
