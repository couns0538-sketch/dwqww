LOSING MATES: THE CONVENTION IS 2N PLIES FOR A LOSS AGAINST 2N-1 FOR A WIN — and my prediction was wrong about it
=====================================================================================================================
Job 43 asked whether the short-PV / wrong-distance hazard of 23-F88/F90 also applies to `mate -N`. It does not apply
in the same way, and the reason is the sign convention, which I got WRONG in the prediction — this job's most useful
output is the convention, stated correctly, plus my own error recorded.

THE CONVENTION, WHICH MY PREDICTION HAD BACKWARDS
--------------------------------------------------
I predicted `mate -2`, reasoning "the engine counts the mater's moves, so a mate 2 plies from now is 2". That is
self-contradictory arithmetic: if N counts the MATER's moves, then a mate delivered by the mater's FIRST move is N=1,
however many plies of the loser's moves precede it. Measured, on both builds and both fixtures: **`mate -1` with a
2-ply PV**, which is exactly consistent with the same rule that governs wins:

  **`mate N > 0`: the side to move needs N of ITS OWN moves to mate → a complete line is 2N-1 plies.**
  **`mate -N < 0`: the OPPONENT needs N of ITS moves to mate the side to move → a complete line is 2N plies.**

Both reduce to "N counts the mating side's moves", so the asymmetry is one ply, not two, and the parity differs: winning
lines are always odd, losing lines always even. A verifier that uses 2N-1 for a loss is wrong by one ply in the
direction that accuses a correct engine of a truncated PV — the same false-positive shape 23-F90 warns about, entered
from the other side. On these fixtures the distinction is sharp: `mate -1` with a 2-ply PV is CORRECT, and my
prediction would have called it a one-ply shortfall.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE F88/F90 HAZARD, SCOPE STATED
-----------------------------------------------------
The won-mate hazard (score wrong about its own distance; PV too short to mate at the first depth) is **not reproduced
here and cannot be, on these fixtures** — with one legal move there is nothing to truncate, and the distance has no
room to be revised. So this job does NOT show the hazard is absent for losing mates; it shows the hazard is not
*necessarily* present, and fixes the arithmetic a checker needs. The honest scope sentence: F88/F90 are statements
about won mates on boards where the search has a choice; whether a losing mate's distance can be revised the way W2's
was (mate 13 → mate 10) is **untested**, and my fixtures were deliberately built so that it could not be.
