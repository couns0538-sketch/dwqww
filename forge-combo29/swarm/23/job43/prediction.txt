PREDICTION — SW-23 job 43 (does the short-PV / wrong-distance hazard also apply to LOSING mates, mate -N?)
Written before the first measuring command. Stamped above by `date -u`. Never edited afterwards.

23-F88 and 23-F90 measured only WON mates (`mate N`, N > 0). A GUI that shows a user "mate in -3" carries exactly the
same trust question, and the ply arithmetic is DIFFERENT: for `mate N > 0` the mating side moves last, so a complete
line is 2N-1 plies; for `mate -N` the side to move is being mated, so the opponent delivers the mate on the opponent's
Nth move and a complete line is 2N plies. If I had assumed 2N-1 for both I would have called every complete losing line
"one ply short". That is the trap this job is designed to avoid.

Boards (side to move is LOST, verified by the same replay machinery):
  L1 `1K6/8/8/3R4/8/8/8/k7 b - - 0 1`   (black to move, K+R v K against it)
  L2 `8/5K2/7Q/4k3/8/8/8/8 b - - 0 1`   (black to move, K+Q v K against it)
Depths 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, base build and PC r15, Threads 1, Hash 16, OwnBook false, no PC20 switches. For every cell
reporting `mate -N`: full PV, replay in python-chess from the SAME fen, and whether the final position is checkmate
(with the side to move at that point having no legal move and being in check).

Prediction (each with the range that kills it):
  P1  THE ENGINE REPORTS A NEGATIVE MATE at some depth on each board (it should — the position is lost). KILLED if a
      board never reports a mate score at any depth; that would mean the engine's losing-mate reporting is absent
      where the win is trivially forced, which would itself be the finding.
  P2  FOR A COMPLETE LOSING LINE, PV PLIES = 2N (not 2N-1).         KILLED if a replaying cell has plies != 2N.
  P3  THE SAME FIRST-DEPTH HAZARD APPEARS: at the first depth reporting `mate -N`, the PV is shorter than 2N plies and
      the replay does NOT end in mate; one depth later it does.       KILLED if the first-depth PV already completes
      — in which case the hazard is specific to won mates and F88/F90 must be scoped that way.
  P4  THE DISTANCE IS NOT REVISED for losing mates the way it was on W2: |N| does not change by more than 1 between
      consecutive depths.                                            KILLED if it moves by 2 or more.

If P2-P4 hold, F88/F90 get a scope statement instead of a silent generalisation: the hazard is the same shape on both
signs, the arithmetic differs by one ply, and a verifier must use 2N-1 for wins and 2N for losses — which is exactly
the kind of off-by-one that makes a correct engine look broken and vice versa.
