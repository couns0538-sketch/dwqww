THREE FAILURES, NOW SEPARATED
------------------------------
1. **Reporting a draw where the draw is real and automatic** (R4): both builds correct.
2. **Reporting a draw where the draw is real but claimable** (R1, R3): both defensible; they differ in policy.
3. **Reporting a draw where NO draw exists** (the F112 board, which was never a threefold): that was my fixture, not the
   engine — 23-F122.
And the fourth, which 23-F60 found and this job does not test: **playing a move that loses the win while reporting a
draw.** That remains the only genuinely bad outcome in the family, and it is about the MOVE, not the score.

WHAT THIS ADDS TO THE FAMILY'S WORTH
-------------------------------------
The drawn-root work now has a clean tri-partition: the base's 0-node move is generator index 0 (23-F98), the bail is a
monotone threshold at three true occurrences (23-F125) extended to halfmove 100 (23-F126), and the SCORE is right
whenever the draw is unambiguous. The cost that remains real is 23-F60's: on a middlegame board the unsearched move hung
a piece. So the family's practical weight rests on the MOVE, and the score is largely sound.
