THE 0-NODE MOVE IS THE ENGINE'S FIRST GENERATED MOVE — on 5 of 5 drawn roots, by the engine's own order
==========================================================================================================
Job 48 asked what "its first legal move" mechanically means for the base's 0-node drawn-root move. The answer is
sharp, and getting to it required correcting my own test twice.

THE MEASUREMENT (base build, Threads 1, Hash 16, OwnBook false)
----------------------------------------------------------------
The yardstick is the engine's OWN generation order, from a `go perft 1` divide on the same position. That is a claim
about the engine's generator, so it is the only yardstick that can settle it.

| case | 0-node / served move | index in ENGINE order | 1st move of divide | move lines | nodes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 threefold, white to move | d5d1 | **0** | d5d1 | 19 | 19 |
| D2 threefold, black to move | a1b1 | **0** | a1b1 | 3 | 3 |
| D3 threefold, rook c1 | c1b1 | **0** | c1b1 | 16 | 16 |
| D4 halfmove 150 | d5d1 | **0** | d5d1 | 19 | 19 |
| D5 bare kings (insufficient material) | a1b1 | **0** | a1b1 | 3 | 3 |
| **C1 CONTROL, not drawn** | **d5b5** | **5** | d5d1 | 19 | 19 |

So on every drawn root tested, of three different draw kinds (threefold repetition, fifty-move, insufficient material),
the move returned with 0 nodes is **the first move the engine generates**. The control is what makes this a finding
rather than a tautology: on the same board WITHOUT the draw, the engine searches 7,581 nodes and chooses index **5**,
not index 0 — so the index-0 behaviour is specific to the 0-node path and is not how this build always selects moves.

P1 HELD: every 0-node move was legal in python-chess's set as well (D1 18th of 19 in python-chess order, D2 3rd of 3,
D3 13th of 16, D4 18th of 19, D5 3rd of 3). Legality is confirmed by an independent implementation, not by the engine.
P3 HELD: two fresh processes per drawn root gave the identical move every time (all five).
P4 HELD: the control searched 7,581 nodes.

WHAT P2 ACTUALLY DID, and why its failure was my test's fault
--------------------------------------------------------------
P2 as written said the 0-node move would be "the first move of python-chess's legal list". Measured: it is index 18,
2, 12, 18, 2 — **0 of 5**, so P2 failed. That failure was uninformative because the yardstick was wrong: python-chess's
iteration order is not the engine's generation order, and a claim about the engine's generator cannot be tested against
a different implementation's ordering. The corrected test (`go perft 1`) puts the move at index 0 on all five.

**This is a post-hoc correction of a test, not a pre-registered confirmation, and it is recorded as such.** The
prediction file was committed and pushed at 01:32:14Z before any measurement and still says what it says; the
correction is here, and 23-F98 reports the finding with the correction attached rather than presenting the perft result
as though it had been predicted.

TWO DRIVER NEAR-MISSES, both of which could have produced a silent wrong answer
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. **The perft summary line is `nodes 19`, not `Nodes searched`.** My first driver waited for the latter, blocked until
   killed, and produced nothing. A hang is visible; a driver that had read ZERO divide lines and then reported "the
   move is not in the engine's order" would have looked exactly like a finding. The saved driver asserts that the move
   lines it parsed equal the `nodes` total and prints `HARD FAILURE` if they disagree, so an unparsed divide cannot be
   read as an empty one.
2. **Reading a divide requires a nonzero-population check**, for the same reason. Both are instances 1/18 of the family
   applied to a new output format — the same lesson that a new format is a new parser and needs its own proof.

THE FINDING, in one sentence for an area-7 code-reading seat
-------------------------------------------------------------
On a drawn root the un-repaired build returns the **first move of its generated list**, unsearched, with a `cp 0` score —
so on these boards the quality of the played move is entirely a property of **move-order generation**, which makes
23-F60's "the 0-node move can lose" a statement about generator order rather than about search. I have NOT read the
code that does this and I do not claim to know why index 0 is chosen (no TT entry, no ordering step, or an early return
before ordering are all consistent with the observation).

LIMITS: base build only (the PC's 0-node path differs by construction — it searches 104/56,153 nodes and so has no
0-node move to explain); five positions, all K+R/K+Q/bare-king endgames; `go perft 1` order may itself differ from
whatever ordering a search would impose, which is exactly why the control's index 5 matters.
