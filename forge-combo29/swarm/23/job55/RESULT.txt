ADDENDA, added after review

1. WHAT WOULD SETTLE THE ROOT-SPECIFIC QUESTION — stated as a PROPERTY rather than a fixture.
   The requirement is a board where a repetition cycle has MORE THAN ONE RETURN PATH: enough material that a shuttle
   can repeat an INTERMEDIATE position, then LEAVE that loop, and land on a position that has never occurred. Knight or
   bishop plus pawns is the shape that satisfies it (the shuttle returns to a non-root square, and a pawn push makes a
   genuinely fresh position). A K+R v K endgame cannot: with two kings and one rook every 4-ply shuttle necessarily
   returns to the root, so no fresh final position is reachable after the loop — which is exactly why T1 was impossible
   on the sparse board. On such a board, BOTH cells are required in one run: T1 (searched position occurs once, an
   intermediate >= 3, and the bail STILL fires) and T2 (root 3x, bail fires). If T1 searches while T2 bails, the bail is
   root-specific. If both bail, it is history-wide. If T2 does not bail (as on the F112 board), nothing is concluded —
   which is the state this job ended in.

2. THE FIRST T1 IS INSTANCE 22 IN ITS STRONGEST FORM — the comparator error that would have BROADENED a defect.
   I counted occurrences of my STARTING fen rather than of the POSITION BEING SEARCHED. The consequence was not a
   missed finding but an OVER-CLAIM: the wrong comparator made a root-specific bail look like a history-wide one, i.e.
   it pointed toward a bigger public claim than the engine supports. The earlier comparator faults in this session
   (23-F104 x2) produced false FAILURES, which are self-limiting because they make you look again; this one produced a
   false REFUTATION IN THE ENGINE'S DISFAVOUR, which is the direction that survives review. That asymmetry is why it
   belongs in the report at full strength.
