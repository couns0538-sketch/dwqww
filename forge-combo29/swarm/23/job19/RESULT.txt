IS THE ~20 % CLOCK-DISAGREEMENT RATE STABLE? — yes, and every disagreement sits at a depth gap of 4+ plies
========================================================================================================
Second sample: 30 DIFFERENT fresh quiet positions (excluding every FEN used in job10 and job17/18), PC r15 + PC20,
Threads 1, Hash 16, OwnBook false, fresh process per search, both clocks run twice.

| measure | first sample (23-F55, n=20) | second sample (n=30) | prediction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 s vs 6 s move disagreement | 4 of 20 = 20 % | **4 of 30 = 13 %** | P1 held (10-33 %) |
| 1 s reaches a lower depth | 20 of 20 | **30 of 30** | P2 held |
| determinism within each setting | 0 disagreements | **0 and 0** | P4 held |

THE RATE REPLICATES within its interval: 13 % against 20 %, on a sample that shares no positions with the first.
So "a fast control chooses a different move on roughly a sixth to a fifth of quiet positions" is supported, while I
still do not claim a precise general rate — two samples give a band, not a constant.

P3 HELD AND IS THE INTERESTING PART: every one of the 4 disagreements sits where the 6 s clock reached at least **4
plies deeper** than the 1 s clock (gaps of 6, 6, 5 and 4 plies). The positions with a small depth gap had 0
disagreements. So the disagreement is not random — it is what happens when the two clocks end at materially
different depths. That is a mechanism rather than a rate, and it is consistent with 23-F26's finding that the move
depends on the MARGIN: a 4-6 ply depth difference is enough to overturn a close call, a 1-2 ply difference is not.

the margin finding but was not tested by DESIGN (I did not select for depth gaps), so it is an observation across
34 positions rather than a controlled experiment.
