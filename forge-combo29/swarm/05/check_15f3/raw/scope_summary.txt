SCOPE OF `DeadMaterial true` — final, evidence-backed
=====================================================
Earlier in this folder I wrote two claims that my own material check (raw/material_check.txt) then DISPROVED,
because several of my hand-typed FENs did not contain the material I had labelled them with. Both are withdrawn:
  WITHDRAWN: "same material, different treatment" — I had no pair of same-signature positions behaving differently.
  WITHDRAWN: "the switch zeroes a three-knight position, which is a rule-level error" — that row was KNNk, not KNNNk.

The controlled test (raw/knn_same_material.txt) asserts every FEN's python-chess piece signature before use:

| material | n positions | zeroed by DeadMaterial true | kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| KNNk  (white K + 2 knights, black K) | 8 | **8 of 8** (cp 216..326 -> cp 0) | 0 |
| KNNNk (white K + 3 knights, black K) | 3 | **0 of 3** (cp 648/399/624 kept, two even rose slightly) | 3 |

What this establishes:
 1. With DeadMaterial true, EVERY KNNk position becomes exactly cp 0, across eight quite different placements
    (knights together, split a1/h1, centre, a8/b8, h8/g8, e4/f4, d5/e5, black king in the centre). That is what
    "the material decides" looks like — the position does not matter.
 2. With the same option, no KNNNk position is zeroed. Three knights CAN force mate, so this is CORRECT chess
    knowledge, and it means the switch is not a blanket "lots of knights = dead" rule.
 3. So the switch behaves like a pure material key for EXACTLY two knights, and it is correct about three knights.
    I have no evidence of a misfire in either set.

This SUPPORTS seat 15's 15-F3 (the option zeroes KNN v K) and adds the scope: exactly two knights yes, three
knights no. It does NOT support any claim that the switch is wrong — my earlier attempt to show that was my error,
not the engine's.
