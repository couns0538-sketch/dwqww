CONCLUSION (read off the table above, no mechanism claimed)
  bare K+R vs K                        -> boundary TRACKS depth (95, 93, 91, 89 for d6..d12)
  the same + ONE black pawn on h2       -> boundary FLAT at 98 for d6..d12
So a single pawn is enough to change the boundary from depth-tracking to flat. The castling variant could not be
tested: python-chess calls "1K6/8/8/3R4/8/8/8/k7 w KQkq - 0 1" invalid (castling rights with no rooks on the
original squares), so that row was skipped rather than forced.

This matches the earlier pawn-stripping test from the other direction: seat 25's middlegame (12 pawns) was flat at
99, and removing all 12 pawns made it flat at 100 — so removing pawns did NOT restore depth-tracking there. Taken
together the two experiments say: bare endgames track depth, positions with a pawn do not, but stripping pawns from a
middlegame does not turn it back into a depth-tracking position. I therefore have TWO groups of observations and no
single property that explains both, and I am not going to invent one.

What I can state honestly for S-F14:
 - with a pawn on the board: boundary flat (98 here, 99 on seat 25's middlegame, 100 on the pawnless middlegame)
 - with no pawn: boundary tracks depth on all four bare endgames tested (d6..d12 -> 94/92/90/88 and similar)
 - a middlegame with its pawns removed stays flat, so "pawns" alone is not the whole story.
