Two predictions failed and I say why rather than quietly dropping them:
- 8: I predicted at most 200 book answers because I imagined the fresh R positions would dominate the pool and
  the book only knows opening lines. In fact the grammar draws `position fen <random R>` and `startpos` in
  roughly equal measure, and every GAME search (`go wtime`, `go movetime`) from startpos or an early R board
  can be a book hit. 786 is 13.6 %, consistent with run 1's 13.1 %, so the number is stable and my band was
  simply wrong.
- 9: the same cause. 1,072 of 5,779 searches (18.6 %) were unbounded/ponder/opponent-clock-only and had to be
  stopped by the driver. My 400-900 band assumed the same mix as my first smoke test, not the full set.

## suspicions, not proven

- The 468 bestmoves with no `info depth` line are still not individually explained. The measured reason for
  the class is the node budget (`go nodes 10` produces exactly that on startpos), and 21 of run 2's `go nodes`
  lines draw a value below 100, but I did not check each one.
- 18.6 % of searches needed the driver to stop them. That is a property of my grammar, not the engine, and it
  means the effective search coverage is lower than the `go` count suggests.

## what I did NOT do

- Did not disable the book (`OwnBook false`), deliberately: the brief's job 1 says nothing about it and
  leaving the default is what a GUI does. The book share is reported so a reader can judge.
- Did not compare node counts between the two runs: they use different position sets, so that would be
  meaningless, and per seat 10's rule node counts are only comparable cold.
- Did not re-derive the illegal-bestmove check with a second independent board model; it is the same
  python-chess model described in the run-1 report.

## what I am NOT sure about

- Whether the 59 `0000` answers are all reachable by a real GUI. They need a `searchmoves` clause with no legal
  move after it, which a GUI sends only when its move list is empty.
- Whether the driver's 30 ms stop for unbounded searches could mask a genuine slow answer. A search that would
  have taken 40 ms is stopped at 30 ms; that changes timing, not legality, and legality is what I check.
