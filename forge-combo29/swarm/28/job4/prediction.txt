# PREDICTION — S28 job 4: do clock searches ever break the engine's own HARD limit?

Committed and pushed ALONE before the first measuring command. Never edited afterwards.

Correction 2 found that 2 of 40 `movestogo 1` searches at `wtime 1000` exceeded the engine's own hard
plan of 792 ms. That is the only place in my clock work where the engine appears to break a bound it
set itself, so it deserves a job of its own rather than a footnote.

The claim to test: **for a clock search, the engine never lets the elapsed time exceed
`hard_ms = min(available*hard_percent/100, soft*hard_soft_multiple)`, clamped to `available`.**

| # | measurement | predicted | range that KILLS it |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | overrun rate at `movestogo 1` wtime 1000, over hard 792 ms | above 0 % and below 20 % | 0 % (my earlier 2/40 was noise) or above 20 % |
| R2 | the largest overrun is a small multiple of hard, not unbounded | < 1.5 × hard = 1188 ms | above 2 × hard |
| R3 | overruns do NOT happen at `wtime 10000 movestogo 1` (hard 7912 ms), because there is more slack | 0 of N | more than 0 |
| R4 | overruns do NOT happen at `movetime 500` (hard = plan, and job 1/correction 1 saw 0) | 0 of N | more than 0 |
| R5 | overruns are concentrated on positions where one iteration is long relative to the budget | yes: median nodes higher on overrun searches | no relation |
| R6 | overrun searches still return a legal bestmove and never exceed the GUI's clock | yes | illegal move or above wtime |

Reasoning: in a clock search the stop is checked at node boundaries inside an iteration, so a search
should be able to stop at the hard limit. If it cannot, it means the check happens only at iteration
boundaries for clock searches too, and an iteration that starts just under hard can run far past it.
R2's kill at 2x keeps the test honest: an unbounded overrun would be a much worse bug than a 10 % one.
60 positions per cell this time (three times job 3's sample), because 2/40 is too thin to characterise.
