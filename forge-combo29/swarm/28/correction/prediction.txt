Committed and pushed ALONE before the first measuring command of this correction. Never edited afterwards.
Written 2026-11-16 ~21:37 UTC, after reading seat 16's refutation of my S-F1.

Seat 16 says: the M-O subtraction exists and `go movetime 10` gives 1 ms, but the stop is the end of
the last whole ITERATION and undershoots the plan; `movetime 50` stops at 26-28 ms at BOTH overhead 0
and overhead 10; `movetime 200` gives 167 ms at both.

My job 1 measured the opposite for overhead: 50.6 ms at overhead 0 and 40.5 ms at overhead 10, a clean
10 ms shift, with a max of 41.6. If seat 16 is right, my numbers must have been an artefact. What could
make them so? My job-1 harness waited for `bestmove` and timed `go`..`bestmove` on the wall clock. That
measures the true stop. So either seat 16 and I are measuring different things (their "elapsed engine
time" is the `time` field of the last info line, mine was wall), or one of us has a harness bug.

Grid: movetime {50, 100, 200, 500} x MoveOverhead {0, 10, 25, 45}, 30 positions per cell, recording
BOTH the wall time and the engine's own reported `time`, plus the last completed depth.

Reasoning: my job-1 numbers were wall times with a 0.05 ms run-to-run agreement, so I do not expect them
to move. But seat 16's claim that BOTH overheads stop at 26-28 ms is a claim about the engine's OWN
reported time, and if the engine stops early and reports early, my wall measurement would have shown it
too. So I predict either a real disagreement about the engine's behaviour (P3 holds) or that seat 16
measured `time` from the last info line and confused that with the stop time. P7 is my honest admission
of seat 16's real point: iterative deepening cannot stop at the plan, so some undershoot is expected —
the question is its size and whether the overhead shifts it.
