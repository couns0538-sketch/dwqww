# PREDICTION — S28 correction 2 (S-F2, S-F6, S-F9 re-checked for the iteration-granularity confound)

Committed and pushed ALONE before the first measuring command of this correction. Never edited afterwards.

Seat 16's confound, restated: any conclusion of the form "the engine used X ms of a Y ms plan" is
dominated by iteration granularity, not by the plan. Correction 1 established the sharper rule:
**the STOP equals the plan to within 1-2 ms, while the engine's own reported `time` (last completed
iteration) sits 15-30 % below it.** So a claim is sound only if I name WHICH quantity I measured.

Three findings to re-check:

**S-F2** — "82 of 200 searches (41 %) at `wtime 1000 winc 10` run past `wtime/30 + winc` (43.3 ms);
largest 201 ms."
The confound would be: 43.3 ms is MY invented reference, not the engine's plan. The engine's actual plan
is soft = 990/22 + 7 = 52 ms, hard = 260 ms. So "past 43.3" was never a claim about the engine's plan.

**S-F6** — "`wtime 1600 winc 1000` (2600 ms for the move) searches median 604 ms while `wtime 2600
winc 0` searches median 92 ms; ratio 0.15x."
Here both sides are ACHIEVED times, so the ratio is like-for-like and the confound cannot create it —
but the magnitude can be inflated by iteration granularity on the FAST side (92 ms may be one iteration
short). The plan numbers (822/954 vs 1584/2640) must be given beside the achieved ones.

**S-F9** — "at `wtime 1000 movestogo 1` the engine uses only 32 % of the clock, stopping at 65 % of its
own soft plan (323 of 495 ms)."
This is exactly the shape seat 16 warns about: 323 vs 495 could be one iteration short of the plan and
nothing more.
