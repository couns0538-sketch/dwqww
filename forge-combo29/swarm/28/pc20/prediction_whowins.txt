# PREDICTION — S28: of the 11 equal-depth disagreements, whose move is better?

Committed and pushed ALONE before the first measuring command. Never edited afterwards.
Written 2026-11-16 ~22:39 UTC.

## The design, and why this is the only fair form of it
S-F28 found 11 of 40 positions where the PC20 switch set and the base tree choose DIFFERENT moves at the
same fixed depth 20. Agreement cannot rank them, so this re-searches each disputed move deeper and compares.

Fairness rules I will follow:
- **One build judges both moves.** All re-searches use the BASE build, so the judge is identical for both
  sides and no build advantage leaks into the verdict.
- **Same depth for both moves in a pair** (`go depth 18`), so the comparison is like-for-like.
- **Same position after the move, opponent to move**, which is the definition of comparing two moves.
- **Scores converted to WHITE's point of view.** The engine reports score relative to the side to move;
  after a move the opponent is to move, so that score is negated for White. Without this the comparison is
  systematically backwards for one side of every pair. Mate scores are mapped to a large sentinel that
  orders correctly (`mate N` for the side to move -> a very large positive for that side).

## What I expect
Most of these 11 positions are decided or clearly better for one side (several are near-endgame or
materially lopsided), and in such positions two moves often transpose to a similar eval. So I expect a
MIXED result with small gaps rather than a clean pattern.

| # | measurement | predicted | range that KILLS it |
|---|---|---|---|
| X1 | positions where the PC20 move scores HIGHER than the base move by >= 20 cp | 3-6 of 11 | 0-1 (base better) or 9-11 (PC20 clearly better) |
| X2 | positions where the gap is < 20 cp (effectively equal) | 3-6 of 11 | 0 |
| X3 | the median absolute gap across the 11 | 15-60 cp | above 150 cp |
| X4 | any pair where the two moves transpose to an identical position | 0-2 | more than 4 |
| X5 | a clear one-sided pattern (8 or more of 11 to one build) | NO | yes |
| X6 | every re-search completes and returns a legal bestmove | yes | any failure |

Reasoning for X5 = no: the two builds share the same evaluation and the same search code apart from the
four switches; the switches change ordering and pruning, not the static evaluation, so at equal depth they
should mostly reach the same conclusion and differ on genuinely close calls. A clean 8-of-11 pattern would
surprise me and would point at something systematic in the switch set, which is exactly what a match would
then confirm. If the result IS mixed, I will say so and stop on this thread as instructed.

## What I will not claim
Re-searching each move deeper on one build is evidence about these eleven positions at depth 18. It is not
a strength measurement, not an Elo estimate, and not a substitute for a match. A mixed result will be
reported as "no pattern at this depth on this sample", not as "the sets are equal".
