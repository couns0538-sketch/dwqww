VERDICT: 30-F59's MOVE CHOICE claim does NOT reproduce on my build, though its SCORE GAP figure does.

CLAIM 1 - "PC20 gives f3g3 at depths 10-16, switching to f3g2 only at depth 18": REFUTED here.
  MY RUN: PC20 chooses **f3g2 at EVERY depth 10, 12, 14, 16, 18 AND 20** (cp 1770/1790/1940/2019/2031/2187).
  The f3g3 move never appears at any depth I tested. S14 also chooses f3g2 throughout, so there is no PC20-vs-S14
  divergence on the MOVE at all on this board in my run.

CLAIM 2 - "f3g2 is 190-230 cp better in isolation at low depth": CONFIRMED, and slightly wider than claimed.
  Isolated scores, PC20: f3g3 vs f3g2 -> +231 (d12), +239 (d14), +198 (d16), +100 (d18).
  S14: +190 (d12), +117 (d14), +189 (d16), +187 (d18).
  So the 200+ cp gap is real at depths 12-16 - but the ENGINE DOES NOT FALL FOR IT: it picks the better move
  everywhere. That is a stronger outcome than their finding describes.

WHY THE MISMATCH IS PLAUSIBLE RATHER THAN MY ERROR: they name no build hash in the finding line, and their
`check17/out/settle_pos29.json` shows the data they collected. Two ways the difference could arise:
  (a) a different binary (their earlier notes reference a patched build in some jobs);
  (b) a different move-ordering tie: at +198 cp the gap is large, so a tie is unlikely, which argues for (a).
I could not determine which, so this is recorded as NOT REPRODUCED on my build, not as proof seat 30 erred - the same
register I used for 27-F12.

WHAT SURVIVES AND IS USEFUL: the isolated-gap measurement itself is a good instrument, and my table shows the gap
SHRIKES with depth on this board (+231/+239/+198/+100 for PC20) - which is the opposite direction from "PC20 is worse
at low depth and self-corrects". Here a low-depth search would be MORE likely to pick the better move than a deep one,
if the gap is what drives the choice. That is the interesting number for the owner's self-play depths: at depth 7-8 the
isolated gap is widest, yet the engine still finds f3g2.

Therefore:

    is the definition in FABLE_TO_swarm_5);
