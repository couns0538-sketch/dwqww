VERDICT: the "gap shrinks with depth" observation does NOT generalise - and the second-board test was the right way
to find that out. Five boards, MultiPV 2, depths 10..18, PC20/Threads 1/Hash 16:
  S-F39 board   : gap 132 -> 104   SHRINKS
  G/1 middlegame: gap  33 ->  10   SHRINKS
  G/2 middlegame: gap  92 ->  84   FLAT
  G/5 endgame   : gap 332 -> 626   **GROWS**
  G/16 endgame  : gap  66 ->  17   SHRINKS
So SHRINKS 3 | GROWS 1 | FLAT 1. The direction is a property of the POSITION, not of depth.

WHY THE ONE-DEPTH OBSERVATION LOOKED GENERAL: on the S-F39 board I had compared a specific alternative move (f3g3)
rather than the engine's own second line, and I had four depths. On a board where the second line's identity CHANGES
between depths (G/1 switches from e5e4 to g7g6 as best; G/16 shows three different best moves h6h5/c6b5/c6d6 across
five depths) the "gap" is comparing different pairs at different depths, so its trend is partly an artefact of WHICH
moves are being compared. That is a real limitation of the MultiPV-gap instrument, not of the boards.

WHAT SURVIVES FROM S-F39: the specific measurement on the correct arm - isolated f3g2 vs f3g3 on that board gives
+231/+239/+198/+100 at depths 12/14/16/18, with the SAME two moves throughout. That is clean because the pair is
fixed. What does NOT survive: the generalisation "the margin is widest at shallow depths". It holds on that board and
on 3 of 5 sampled here, but G/5 endgame grows by 294 cp over the same range.

RECOMMENDED STATEMENT: on a given board, the margin between the engine's choice and a FIXED alternative move may
shrink, grow or stay flat with depth; it is not a depth law. Any claim of the form "shallow searches have less/more
margin" needs per-board measurement, not an assumption.
