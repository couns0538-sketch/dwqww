A: r4rk1/pp1b1ppp/4p3/3pP3/8/P3P3/1P2N1PP/R2R2K1 w - - 99 1

1. white's quiet move a1a2 puts the clock at 100 for black:
   FEN r4rk1/pp1b1ppp/4p3/3pP3/8/P3P3/RP2N1PP/3R2K1 b - - 100 1   clock 100
   python-chess: is_game_over(claim_draw=True) = True
   -> at clock 100 with black to move, the game IS drawn unless black is checkmated.
      is_checkmate = False

2. can BLACK avoid the draw? black must make a move; if black plays a capture or pawn move,
   the clock resets and the game continues:
   black's reset moves: 11 ['h7h6', 'g7g6', 'f7f6', 'b7b6', 'a7a6', 'd5d4']

3. so what should the engine say at clock 99?
   clock 0  : ('cp', -155), bestmove d1c1
   clock 99 : ('cp', 0), bestmove a1a2

VERDICT: white at clock 99 can reach clock 100 with a quiet move, and the game is then DRAWN
(the fifty-move rule, not a claim either side can refuse once 100 quiet half-moves have passed).
So `cp 0` at clock 99 is CORRECT for white - it is a real drawing resource, and the clock-0 value of
-155 cp is what white AVOIDS by taking it. The engine reporting 0 here is RIGHT.

What is then WRONG is narrower: the engine reports the draw with a 1-ply PV and, in the earlier
severity test, plays a move that is 40-999 cp worse when the clock is NOT actually drawn.
