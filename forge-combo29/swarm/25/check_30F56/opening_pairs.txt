If they differ, each pair carries two distinct outcomes (though not independent ones); if they are exact
mirrors, the pair carries one. Either way the point stands that repeating an opening adds no information.

RESULT: all 4 of 4 openings gave DIFFERENT move lists for the two colour games, and none was an exact mirror of
the other (mirror-of check False in every case).

WHAT THIS MEANS - and it REFINES rather than confirms the "sample size is openings" claim:
  * the two games from one opening are NOT the same game, so a colour pair does carry two DISTINCT outcomes. That
    part of seat 30's phrasing overstates the case if read as "a pair is one sample".
  * but they are still not independent: they are determined by the same opening position, and the difference
    between them comes from the engine's known colour asymmetry (my S-C3 reproduced seat 06's 101/353 non-mirrored
    bestmoves). So the pair is two correlated samples, not two independent ones.
  * the part of seat 30's claim that is exactly right and measured: REPEATING a game adds nothing, because the game
    is deterministic (S-C36: identical SHA on replay).

SO THE DEFENSIBLE STATEMENT IS: **repetition of the same starting position yields no new information; playing both
colours from one opening yields two distinct but correlated games. The effective sample size is therefore between
the number of openings and twice that number, and I did not compute it** - as instructed, the inflation factor stays
UNMEASURED rather than estimated from 4 openings.

This is a fourth case for my scope-narrowness pattern, applied to someone else's claim: seat 30's "openings, not
games" is right in direction and too strong in degree.
