Reading, three facts:
1. **MPV1 is completely unaffected by Hash** - depth 16 / 172,312 nodes at every Hash from 16 to 1024 (nodes/ms
   1,657 -> 1,401, a mild cache effect). So a large Hash alone is harmless.
2. **MPV3 at Hash 256 spends 290 ms to search 622 nodes** - 2 nodes/ms against 1,566 for MPV1. The clock budget is
   being consumed without searching. That is exactly what a large, repeatedly-cleared TT costs.
3. **The degradation is monotone in Hash at MPV3**: nodes/ms 559 (H16) -> 183 (H64) -> 2 (H256), depth 13 -> 9 -> 4.
So the three-condition statement is now mechanistic, not just descriptive: **clock + MultiPV > 1 + large Hash, where
the large Hash makes the between-line TT clear consume the clock budget.** The clean Hash-16 ladder (16/15/13/11 at
MPV1/2/3/4) is the control proving the MultiPV ladder itself is sound and only the clear is at fault.

PREDICTION for the one-statement patch: it should work by REMOVING the redundant between-lines clear, not by
changing MultiPV behaviour - i.e. after the patch, MPV3+Hash256 should show a nodes/ms close to MPV1's, not close to 0.
That is a falsifiable check for whoever holds the patch.
