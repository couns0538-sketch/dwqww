*** THIS CORRECTS MY S-C21. The collapse is HASH-SPECIFIC, not a general clock x MultiPV defect. ***

Same position, same clock `go wtime 5000`, 4 fresh runs per cell:
  Hash  16: MPV1 depth 16 (172,312 nodes, 1.0x) | MPV2 15 (208,068) | MPV3 13 (118,566) | MPV4 11 (72,171)
            -> a clean, STABLE ladder. MultiPV costs 1-2 plies per extra line, exactly as expected.
  Hash 256: MPV1 depth 16 (172,312, 1.0x)       | MPV2 depths [4,5] (382-950, 2.5x spread)
            MPV3 depth [4] (622)                | MPV4 depths [2,4] (141-766, 5.4x spread)
            -> COLLAPSE: 16 plies become 2-5, and the answer is unstable run to run.
  `go depth 12` at Hash 256: MPV1..4 all depth 12, exact node counts -> deterministic limits unaffected.

So the defect needs ALL THREE: a CLOCK, MultiPV > 1, and a LARGE Hash. Remove any one and it is either stable or
merely costs a ply or two. My S-C21 said "the clock x MultiPV pair" - that was measured at Hash 256 only, and I
generalised it one factor too far. Corrected statement: **clock x MultiPV x large-Hash**.

WHY this is plausible: with MultiPV > 1 the engine clears the TT between lines (seat 12 measured that), and a large
Hash makes that clear expensive; the time manager then sees the budget spent and stops at whatever shallow depth it
has. At Hash 16 the clear is cheap so the ladder survives.

PRACTICAL: the owner's matches use MultiPV 1, so the match path is untouched (MPV1 is exact at both Hashes). The
exposure is analysis at MultiPV 3-4 with a large Hash, where the engine will silently return depth 2-5 instead of
~13-16. That is worth telling anyone doing analysis, and it is the real content of seat 30's finding.
