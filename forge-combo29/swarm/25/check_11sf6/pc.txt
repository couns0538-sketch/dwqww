The distinction I missed when I filed S-F6: `mate 0` and `mate 1` are DIFFERENT signals in the same encoding -
mate 1 means "mate is available in one move" and comes with a real move, while mate 0 means "already mated" and
comes with 0000. So `mate 0` is not an undefined token; under UCI's `score mate y` = "mate in y moves for the side
to move", y = 0 is the natural encoding of "mated now", and 0000 is the only sensible reply. My "mate 0 cannot
occur at a non-terminal node" observation is fair but it is a property of the terminal case, not a fault.

S-F6 IS WITHDRAWN as a defect. What remains true and is recorded: `mate 0` is unusual in that most engines report a
large centipawn value for a mated root, so a consumer that assumes "mate means a positive/negative integer >= 1"
must handle 0 - but that is a consumer note, not an engine fault. Seat 21's 21-F11 was withdrawn for the same reason.
