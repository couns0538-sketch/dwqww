HYPOTHESIS SUPPORTED on this sample: the first mate distance reported was also the final one. So a shallow
search appears to report the SHORTEST mate it can see, which is why a right-length PV has always been
correct - the accept side is near-exact here.

RESULT: HYPOTHESIS SUPPORTED on this sample - **0 of 15 boards** showed the reported mate distance falling as depth
grew. Every board's FIRST reported mate distance was also its FINAL one at depth 16, and every series was monotone
(never decreasing).

THE PATTERN IN THE DATA, which is the mechanism the hypothesis predicted:
  8/8/8/4k3/8/2K5/5Q2/8 w   : no mate at d6..d14, then **mate 7 at d16** - the mate APPEARS at the depth that first
                              sees it, and it appears at its true distance immediately
  2Q5/8/8/4k3/8/8/8/K6R w   : no mate at d6/d8, **mate 5 from d10 on**
  8/8/4K3/8/5k2/Q7/8/8 w    : no mate at d6..d10, **mate 5 from d12 on**
So a mate does not arrive as "mate 9, then 7, then 5" - it arrives as **nothing, then the true distance**. That is
exactly why a shallow search's mate number is reliable: it either has not found the mate (reports cp) or has found it
at the correct depth.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE FILTER, stated with the ceiling intact:
  * catches every observed failure (SHORT 4, ok 8, right-length-not-mate 0);
  * accept side has survived **21 boards** (S-C32 x2) with no counter-example;
  * and now the MECHANISM is measured on 15 more boards: 0 cases of a falling mate distance.
Still not a proof: this tests mate-in-N reporting on mostly simple endgames, where a horizon that finds a mate finds
the shortest one. A position where a long mate is visible before a short one - possible in principle - would still be
a blind spot, and I did not construct one.
