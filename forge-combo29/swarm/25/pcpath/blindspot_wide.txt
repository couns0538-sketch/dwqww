So the filter's blind spot is still NOT demonstrated. That is a materially stronger null than S-C32 alone: two
different depths, two different position generators, 21 boards, and not one case where a PV passed the length test
while misstating the distance.

WHY IT MAY BE STRUCTURALLY HARD TO TRIGGER (hypothesis, not proven): a shallow search that finds a mate appears to
find the SHORTEST mate its horizon can see, and a horizon that can see a mate in k can see the shorter mates too -
so "right length + checkmate" may essentially IMPLY "correct distance" for this engine's mate bookkeeping. If that
holds, the filter is stronger than "catches every observed failure": it may be near-exact on the accept side too.

WHAT I STILL DO NOT CLAIM: correctness. Two failed attempts are not a proof, and the hypothesis above is a
hypothesis. The honest statement remains: the filter catches every observed failure; its accept side has survived 21
boards without a counter-example; that is evidence, not proof.
