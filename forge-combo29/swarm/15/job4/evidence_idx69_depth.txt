Truth, two independent solvers: MATED_IN_PLIES 6  => mate -3. CONFIRMED:
  $ python3 solvemate2.py 'k7/6R1/5N2/p4K1P/P7/8/8/8 b - - 0 1' 12  -> MATE/MATED_IN_PLIES 6
  $ python3 matedbf.py   'k7/6R1/5N2/p4K1P/P7/8/8/8 b - - 0 1' 12  -> MATED_IN_PLIES 6

WHAT THIS SHOWS, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT
- The fault is a HORIZON effect, not a permanent misevaluation: at depth 16 the engine finds the
  mate in 6 plies exactly, and the PV ends in `h7h8q` checkmate, which is the truth.
- It is NOT a "the position is too hard" excuse either: seldepth is 24 in all three lines, i.e. the
  same quiescence reach, yet 14 says 18 plies and 16 says 6. The depth-14 number is not a slightly
  pessimistic estimate of a hard problem; it is a fabricated distance for a mate it can see in 6
  plies one iteration later.
- The 15-ply PV at depth 14 is 3 plies SHORT of the 18 it promises, and at depth 16 the PV is
  exactly the 6 plies the score claims. So "PV shorter than the promised mate" is the observable
  signature of this fault, and it is detectable without a solver.
- I did NOT identify the mechanism (root cause unknown). It is consistent with a mate score coming
  back from the transposition table at a ply that does not match, but I have no evidence for that and
  do not claim it.

PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE FOR A GUI OR A TEST
A depth-limited `go depth 14` answer of `mate -9` with a 15-ply PV here is wrong by 6 moves, and the
same engine at depth 16 is right. Any harness asserting mate distances must either search deeper than
it trusts or check PV-length-vs-score consistency, which catches both of my instances (idx 69, idx
192) and none of the 8 correct mates in job 1b.
