=== NOTE ON THE PLY NUMBERS (honest) ===
moveline.py reports "opponent mates in 7 plies" for idx 192, whereas solvemate2.py/matedbf.py
reported MATED_IN_PLIES 8 for the same root. The difference is one ply of bookkeeping: moveline
measures from AFTER the loser's move (opponent to move, mate in 7), while the solvers measure from
the root (loser to move, mated in 8 = 1 + 7). They agree; I state the convention rather than
present two numbers that look contradictory. Same for idx 69: 5 plies after black's move
= MATED_IN_PLIES 6 from the root.

=== VERDICT ===
REPORTING FAULT, not a search fault, on the evidence of both instances.
- The engine's move is a correct (here: the only, or an equally good) best defence in both positions.
- The engine's SCORE and PV are wrong: it says 18 plies where the truth is 6 (idx 69) / 8 (idx 192),
  and its PV is 15 plies in both cases, short of the 18 it promises.
- Consistent with, and now explained by, 15-F11: one iteration later (depth 16) the same engine
  gives the true mate -3 with a 6-ply PV ending in checkmate.

LIMIT OF THIS EVIDENCE, stated so nobody over-reads it:
Neither position offers a CHOICE among moves of different loss length, so I have shown that the move
played is correct, not that the engine always picks the longest defence when a shorter one exists.
That distinction matters: a wrong distance could still mis-rank moves in a position where two moves
lose in different numbers of plies. I did not construct such a position; it is the obvious next test.
