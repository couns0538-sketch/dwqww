Offline check of the omitted opening history

Use python-chess 1.11.2 (the chess distribution) and a local Git repository
containing Job 22 commit 040834de294a31f81a3d6aa54a605af692a4fcb4.
Run "python3 repetition_screen.py /path/to/repository". The script reads
the committed L1-r15d games.tsv, openings.tsv and records.txt directly.
The output identifies their hashes. For a shorter trial use --end GAME.

Each search root in R's recorded game has its original opening and actual
played prefix. R's reversible window can include pre-book positions until
the halfmove clock resets. B starts its position-key history at FEN0 and
retains the halfmove clock from the FEN. Position identity here is the
pieces, side to move, castling rights and any legally capturable en-passant
right. The script counts keys strictly before the current search root in
the full reversible window and in the same window clipped at FEN0.

For r15d, search.cpp line 2645 makes the current position the search root.
In board.cpp lines 1402–1413 a position reached during search can count
one prior occurrence at or after that root, or two before it. The upcoming
repetition test in lines 1729–1734 uses the same distinction. Lines
1391–1399 implement the ordinary threefold count, and line 1581 adds the
en-passant file to the position key when a legal en-passant capture exists.
The screen flags roots with at least two matching earlier positions in the
full window and fewer than two after clipping. Earlier positions lost
from before the root cannot change the at-or-after-root matches.

Across 2,000 R games and 232,013 recorded searches, 924 opening positions
had positive halfmove clocks and 1,076 had zero. The reversible window
reached before FEN0 at 1,849 played search roots. Only two roots qualified,
both in game 701704. At plies 9 and 10 the earlier positions at plies 4
and 8 were equal, and B retains only ply 8. The opening moves took the
white queen from d2 to g5 and back and the black bishop from f8 to b4 and
back. Two terminal openings produced no searches.

This positional screen counts opportunities on the recorded R paths. It
cannot establish which hypothetical continuations either search visited,
whether a repetition changed a bound, or whether the change reached a
reported best move or node count. It also omits any indirect effect from
a preceding search whose output happened to agree. No engine was run for
this check.
