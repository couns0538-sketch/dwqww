IdeaS10, seat 031. The plain engine does not already do this. EvalRule50Damp
shrinks a static score from the half-move clock. The SIMD "shuffle" in
search.cpp is a register permute. Neither is a probe that forbids pawn moves
and captures.

Forge's search score is the network plus a correction. There is no pawn-score
number exposed to search, and the idea forbids a second evaluation function.
"Pawn score large and almost flat" is therefore the static eval already stored
on the stack: its magnitude is at least IdeaS10_Margin, and it has moved by at
most IdeaS10_Flat over two plies, while both of those plies were non-capturing
non-pawn moves (the pawn key did not change, so the pawn part of the score is
flat).

The side with the positive static eval is the side that is supposed to be
winning. The probe is a search of IdeaS10_Probe plies in which that side cannot
play a capture, a promotion, or a pawn move. It does not call itself again.
If the probe score stays within IdeaS10_Close of the real score, and the
defender has a quiet non-pawn move or the position is a repetition, the node
score is multiplied by IdeaS10_Shrink/1024, toward 0. Mate scores are left
alone. The check is off by default and the probe is not called, which is why
the off search matches the plain engine node for node.

Defaults, chosen before the on measurement: Margin 80, Flat 40, Probe 2,
Close 30, Shrink 768, MinDepth 4. A tighter flat band looked likely to miss
every opening at depth 12. The probe is short so a depth-12 search stays a
depth-12 search plus a shallow restricted look.

R positions: python-chess, random.Random(3101), at most 80 plies. A game that
reaches a chess ending inside those 80 plies is dropped. Ply 8, then every 7
plies. This seed produced 300 FENs from 28 games and dropped none, because
none of those games had ended by ply 80.
