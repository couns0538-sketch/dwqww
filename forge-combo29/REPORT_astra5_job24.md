## Facts

Each pair set started a fresh official fastchess process. Both players used the stage's executable. The runner specified PC20, the net path, then NNUE activation, followed by the plus or minus vector. Common options set Threads to 1, Hash to 16 and OwnBook to false. Fastchess drew eight seeded EPD openings and played each twice with colors reversed. The saved command arrays and full fastchess logs preserve each invocation. An independent check reconstructed all 179 seeded draws and matched the opening FENs in all 2,854 finished games. [K1 seed and FEN readback](astra5/job24/audit/openings/K1-openings.tsv) The comparison joined games by pair set, round and game within the round, using legal moves in play order. [Runner](astra5/job24/driver/stage24.py) · [First actual command](astra5/job24/R1/j001.command.json) · [PGN comparison](astra5/job24/audit/reconcile_games.py)

## Suspicions

The matching K1 subset is consistent with earlier driver tests. C24 shows why a score-line check alone can miss a change in search behavior. All 16 move lists changed and the summed score stayed the same. [C24 game and result counts](astra5/job24/audit/C24-independent.txt)

## What I did not do

WHAT THIS CANNOT SEE: the PC's own build (clang 23 for Windows, its profile), its trained net, its book (UHO_Lichess_4852_v1.epd), fastchess on Windows, the tune's 40,000 iterations with steps that shrink as they go (yours are one step each, around today's start values), the tune's later values.

## What I am not sure about

These fixed-node games measure reproducibility of the tuning inputs and outcomes under this Linux configuration. The owner's Windows tuning sequence and network could visit different positions and settings.
