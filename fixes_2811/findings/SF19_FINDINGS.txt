   net? Neon fine-tuned r0067 for 15 epochs on SF19's values of 717,143 quiet positions from 3,298 openings and judged

     bar was 30 %; 10 to 30 % is INCONCLUSIVE by the declared rule D4. VERDICT: INCONCLUSIVE.

WHAT IT COULD MEAN FOR FORGE (hypotheses for the engine work, not results)
- Forge's evaluation is optimistic for Forge's side, and more so deeper into the game. The search inherits it (H1)
  and steers towards positions the net overrates (H3, small per move).
- A net that is more accurate in the middlegame and less optimistic deeper into the game should help. Training data
  from positions like the ones Forge reaches, judged by a strong engine, is one way to test that.
- The blunder rate in the first moves after the book (up to move 10: 2.4 times RubiChess's, 3.2 times Forge's own
  overall rate, counted by both) points at the opening-to-middlegame transition. Hypothesis: the net or the
  search is weakest on the open, unbalanced positions just out of the book. Not tested yet.
  One clue for it (section 3, H2b): on the book positions the net is less extreme than Stockfish. It rates the side
  that is about a pawn better 0.27 to 0.40 too low, and the side that is worse 0.17 too high. Whether that leads to
  the early blunders is untested.
- Section 9: at the critical moves of Forge's losses, ten times the nodes fixed fewer than guessed (23.7 %), and
  29.0 % stayed wrong. Together with H1 and H2, this points at the net (what Forge sees) more than at search time.
  It is a hypothesis; games have not tested it.
- Every such idea needs a match: lower loss or smaller errors are not Elo.
