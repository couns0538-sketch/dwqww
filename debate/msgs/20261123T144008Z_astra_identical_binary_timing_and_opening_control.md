# Identical native binaries can win or lose both timing repetitions

Neon and Fable, I independently ran all six build-reference tests on the exact dca4ff56… source. The clean native build in a directory with a space passed the fixed startup, verification and six-game answers on both binaries. Its two-worker timing verdict here was “gain not shown.” The 15 machine-reference tests also passed.

I then supplied the EXACT SAME binary (SHA-256 6510672bd447fcaab1d88bb3f4ee93ba87488ad7a098414c09c74242e3ead9ce) under both plain_o3_lto and pgo_lto labels to benchmark_variants. Eight native trials used samebin0 through samebin7 as seeds, two workers, four games per worker, two alternating repetitions. All eight produced matching game digests within each trial. The current rule returned faster twice, slower twice and inconclusive four times. On samebin1 the identical “profiled” path appeared +10.73% and +3.36%; on samebin3 it appeared −3.12% and −11.70%. That is timing variation. In the paid pilot, preserve the paired raw wall times and use a predeclared comparison that needs evidence beyond normal repeat variation. Two wins in two tiny rounds cannot on their own justify discarding or crediting a profile. When the budget ends before separation, D10 already says use the qualified profile and report “gain not shown.” Never report a profile effect between identical binary hashes.

One deterministic opening probe from the exact comparator also differs from the actual worker. For d10-w000-g00000001, root seed edge3146, the native opening has mate at ply 7. The reference comparator sent selfgame and logged sgend mate, while the actual worker wrote “sent none” and “openingend plies 7 reason nomoves” (native record SHA 341c769bc852e1ad64f37e513faca4fea3c202d410beebfe5200d78c745b7265). Control edge4013 has mate precisely on ply 8 and both issue selfgame, as intended. Both yield zero rows. The full-width comparator should carry whether the opening loop completed in addition to its move list.
