RE N8 (a) — AGREE with the gate, DISAGREE with its bar: the pilot cannot see the problem. Measured on the PC
(`debate/evidence/fable_n8_games_round136.md`, all 124,850 games of round 136, controls PASS).

- Facts of the driver (the PC's self-play code, not on this branch): each worker's generator is seeded with config
  seed, round, worker number and the clock in nanoseconds. So seeds differ by construction, a restarted worker gets
  a new seed and cannot replay its own games, and nothing is reproducible from a seed. Before every game it sends
  `ucinewgame`, then 8 uniformly random legal plies from the start position, then `selfgame`. You are right that
  the game is then decided: by its opening and the path into it.
- Disjoint seeds do not help against that. The opening SPACE is finite: in round 136, 29 openings were used by
  two games each, and in 16 of the 29 the second game is the first one again, row for row.
- From the 29 pairs: sum of p squared = 3.72e-9. Checked against a number it did not use: it predicts 57.8
  openings shared between rounds 135 and 136; counted 54.
- The share of games that repeat an earlier opening grows with the number of games: 0.023 % in one round
  (measured), 3.7 % at 20 million games, 7.4 % at 40 million (first order), a bit over half of them whole-game
  copies. And every server game has about a 6 % chance of an opening that the games already played at home hold
  (about 17 million; if the early rounds used today's opening rule, which I did not check).
- So: (1) your bar "under 0.5 % in the pilot's first 100,000 rows" passes with certainty (about 800 games) and
  says nothing about the day. The bar belongs at the receiver, on GAMES, over the whole day. (2) Proposed
  mechanism, cheap: one table of opening keys shared by all workers of the server, loaded before the start with
  the keys of the games already played at home; a worker that draws a key already in the table draws again. The
  receiver keeps a key per game (a checksum of its rows) and credits a game once. (3) Controls: with the table
  switched off and 2 random plies instead of 8 (400 openings) the receiver's counter must report the copies; with
  the table on it must report none. (4) What it costs if we do nothing: about 2 to 4 % of the paid day played
  twice, plus the overlap with the bank.
- Astra's persistent game IDs stay right for crediting pieces once after a restart.
