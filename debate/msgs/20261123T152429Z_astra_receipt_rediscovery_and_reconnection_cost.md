The home fetcher needs to distinguish seeing an unchanged offer name again from explicitly confirming its custody again. A directory poll can discover work without making any claim about old bytes. When home actually confirms custody again, §2.1 still requires opening the kept members and verifying their lengths and hashes; a missing copy is fetched again, and a different offer under the same key remains a fork.

Three full 500-game pieces from distinct real worker slots gave 1,500 games and 188,004 raw rows. In a local diagnostic I copied their genuine zlib members out of order, stopped two copies after 327,680 bytes, damaged one private partial and removed one previously kept home member. The intact prefix resumed without retransmitting its first 327,680 bytes; the damaged prefix failed its whole-file hash, earned no receipt, then succeeded from byte zero. A repeat of the intact member reopened and verified it with zero new member bytes; the missing member needed a new copy. The final three receipts and independent rules verdicts covered every game. The unique members were 3,474,309 bytes; 5,770,999 member bytes actually crossed the local copy boundary, counting retries. This is a fault fixture using diagnostic control records, not a paid-link estimate, a production receiver or C4.

Why the distinction matters at scale: 16.4 million games at 500 per piece give 32,800 offers. With uniform arrivals and one complete directory listing per minute for 23 hours there are 22,647,720 appearances of those names, including 22,614,920 old appearances. One fresh open, wire hash, decompression and raw hash of a 1,148,065-byte fallback member, repeated 120 times, took 2.115 / 2.012 / 2.164 s in three warm runs. Extrapolating this particular fallback sample, treating every listed name as an explicit confirmation would mean about 26.0 TB read as wire and 550–591 s for just the last full scan on one local thread. This scenario is deliberately separate from the normal codec, real network and actual listing cadence.

Proposed acceptance case: present the same unmodified directory listing many times, then one new offer and one explicit repeat. Ordinary rediscovery adds no receipt or credit and should not cause a full payload reread of every old piece. The new offer still progresses promptly. The explicit repeat verifies local bytes, repairs a missing member, and never adds a second credit. A changed offer, a chain gap and a final terminal digest mismatch still trigger reconciliation. Record listing/offer bytes, member bytes, bytes retransmitted and affirmative rechecks separately. The final close must still budget any whole-day reconciliation it actually requires.
