# Proposed later training diagnostic: hold out whole games before screening recipes

Neon, A17 checks that a training batch mixes games. A separate question is whether a validation row comes from a game already seen in training. On 1,500 real native games, 1,487 nonempty and 188,004 raw rows, I reserved one fifth of whole games by a frozen hash. Among other games I also held out one fifth of the rows. Exactly the same trained model scored both panels.

Three predeclared salts gave 29,646 / 30,265 / 30,143 row-held-out examples. All had a training sibling from the same game. Of those, 29,585 / 30,169 / 30,068 also had a training sibling within two plies. Whole-game holdouts had zero same-game siblings. They still shared 14 / 31 / 36 board-and-turn occurrences with training, so a by-game split alone does not eliminate transpositions. A deliberately leaky game-result lookup recovered all the seen-game targets. A linear piece-square model gave *no resolved difference* under any of the three game-level bootstrap intervals for either raw result or mapped search score. This demonstrates composition and tests the audit. It does not show that the PC trainer is leaking, or that a new recipe plays better.

Proposed trigger: after W8, if an offline validation measure will rank an A19 or later recipe and measured game overlap in its claimed held-out panel is positive, build a game-separated diagnostic panel, with a separately held-out later round where available. Declare the split, per-game weighting and any remaining cross-game position overlap before training. Known-answer pair: a planted row split fails the disjoint-game check, the whole-game split passes, and the exact identity lookup loses its advantage on new games. Run side by side on the same fitted net. Only games with both training seeds can change the chosen recipe. One three-split local audit took 3.50 CPU seconds; any PC/full-store scan must be separately priced. Do you agree with the diagnostic, or would you prefer to keep this as evidence only?
