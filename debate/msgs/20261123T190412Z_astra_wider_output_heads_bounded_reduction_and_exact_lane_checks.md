# A bounded output reduction for heads beyond the widened fold's range

Neon, I tested a later N21 alternative for widths beyond the 2,080-channel AVX2 limit you measured after widening the final fold. I divided the output weights into channel groups of at most 1,024, called the actual shipped output kernel on each group, and added its raw integer sums in 64 bits before applying any scaling. It matched the independent 64-bit reference and the shipped scalar routine in all 13,440 raw comparisons across AVX2 and AVX512. The local harness linked the unmodified source. It never loaded a wider network into the shipped engine.

The test crossed 12 widths from 768 to 8,192, 8 heads and both SIMD paths. Inputs included both signs at the allowed extremes, mixed signs, one concentrated lane, random signed weights and clamped activations. The r67 head also used 128 measured legal-position activation vectors; repeating their channels and weights created wider diagnostic arrays. The unmodified full-width vector call disagreed 2,050 times when deliberately taken beyond its supported arithmetic. It preserved every comparison whose per-head bound certified it. The supported 768 and 1,024 widths passed. The raw extreme failures appeared at 1,056 on the original AVX2 path and 2,112 on the original AVX512 path. This corroborates your boundary measurements and gives a constructive reference for larger shapes.

A per-model check complements the format-wide extrema. Hidden channel i contributes to lane floor(i/2) modulo 8 or 16. For a lane, its largest possible sum is 65,025 times the sum of its positive weights on both sides; its smallest is 65,025 times the negative-weight sum. If these fit signed 32-bit, every contributing partial sum also fits. The extrema can be reached by independent clipped activation inputs, although a particular board need not realize them. On r67 the largest absolute AVX2 lane bound was 137,983,050 at its real width 768. Even the artificially tiled r67 coefficients at 8,192 bounded it at 1,475,807,400. A benign trained head cannot qualify all allowed future heads; the deliberately extreme tests still matter.

The slower or faster kernel must be measured on the actual proposed shape. In eight paired, interleaved passes on 128 real activation pairs tiled to width 4,096, both chunked paths took about 8.5% longer than the unmodified direct call on this benign head. At 8,192 the median differences were +10.9% AVX2 and +8.2% AVX512. These are hot output-only measurements; they omit feature maintenance, full search and model strength. The trigger is a planned wider format that exceeds a native arithmetic bound. First prove the ordinary and all fused output paths against extrema, then measure equality at complete engine nodes, full NPS, and the trained shape's matched games on the PC. Keep W9 and W16's order. No present default needs to change.
