# Trying a second opening move increased variety only on deliberately repeated starts

Neon, I tested the later proposal to sometimes play a near-equal second move early in self-play. Two original r67 cohorts of 8,192 complete games had 2.79% and 2.89% repeated raw piece placements with the same side to move. Each cohort contained more than a million emitted rows. Every cross-game repeated placement appeared after ply 80. Neither reached the proposed 10% trigger. Counting exact network inputs leaves 1,015,053 distinct inputs in 1,047,129 rows and 1,023,359 in 1,057,064. The exact input includes the output bucket and both side-ordered sets of active features. The real home filter and stored-round counts still need measuring.

I then compared three generators on two fresh banks of 512 separate openings. Ordinary self-play cost 0.06408 child CPU hours over both banks. A 20,000-node, two-move probe followed by always choosing first cost 0.06703 hours, as did the same probe occasionally choosing second. Each probe really spent 20,000 nodes; the final visible iteration under-reported its cost. At equal probing cost the change in distinct placements per CPU hour was +0.77% [−0.56, +2.12] and −0.26% [−1.64, +1.14] on the fresh banks. Against ordinary self-play, exploration produced 7.60% and 7.42% fewer distinct placements per CPU hour. The preregistered test asked for at least 5% more in both fresh banks.

As a control, allocate every one of 16 openings eight times on each bank. The same policy now raises distinct placements per core-hour by 44.70% and 48.96%. We know which 112 reservations repeat on each bank before running a game. This would make a misleading benchmark for a normal generator with fresh openings. The quality check was separate. An independent referee compared both moves on every switched root. On the two fresh banks the estimated expected-score loss per requested slot was 0.17 [−0.16, +0.52] and 0.26 [−0.02, +0.56] percentage points. Both upper limits passed the 1.5-point budget; the throughput test still failed.

All 3,584 games and 460,000 emitted rows were replayed. My independent decoder reproduced the distinct-row counts and throughput comparisons from the compressed game records. A contemporary sample also regenerated 512 of 8,192 original games in each cohort with byte-identical whole-game transcripts. The two measured rates were 1.812M and 1.808M distinct placements per process-CPU hour, a difference of −0.24% [−2.17, +1.74]. These are raw candidate positions on this host. On a stronger producer, repeat the test only if the paid, filtered data show the signed duplication or cost trigger. Measure the complete pipeline and compare against ordinary self-play. No change to the current generation policy.
