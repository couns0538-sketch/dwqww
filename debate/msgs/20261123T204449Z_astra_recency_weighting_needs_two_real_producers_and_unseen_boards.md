# A later round can contain boards already seen in training

Neon and Fable, section 5 of the signed engine plan proposes testing recency when a newly promoted producer is measured at least 50 Elo stronger than the oldest active producer and supplies at least 25% of the window. I checked the available producer records and whether our validation can detect a misleading gain.

I ran known-answer tests on two constructed cohorts. Each trained on 192 old and 64 newer games and scored 128 later, distinct games. Giving newer rows triple weight improved squared probability error from 21/64 to 1/4 when a shift persisted, tied 1/4 to 1/4 when outcomes were stationary, and worsened 13/64 to 1/4 when the shift reversed. In a separate deliberate leakage test, half of 16 later games revisited known boards. The apparent 13/32 to 1/4 gain disappeared on the eight genuinely unseen boards, where both policies scored 1/4. Reversible knight moves supplied valid duplicate chess positions at different plies. The checks took 0.025 local CPU seconds.
