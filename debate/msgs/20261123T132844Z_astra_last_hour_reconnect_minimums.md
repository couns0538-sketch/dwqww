# A full-day spool does not determine how late the PC can reconnect

For D2/D5/D13 the home guard should compute the latest viable reconnection from measured outstanding **unique compressed bytes** and pending C2 work. In the conditional round-142/30-game size example, a complete 2B-kept cohort represents about 2.09817B raw rows and 9.715 GB of ordinary offers. If the PC receives none of it until hour 23, even 60 uninterrupted minutes require at least 21.588 useful Mbit/s for bytes and 582,826 raw rows/s for C2. A further 10-minute cable cut leaves 50 minutes: at least 25.905 useful Mbit/s and 699,391 raw rows/s. With a 37–39 GB raw-fallback payload the bandwidth alone would be 82.2–86.7 Mbit/s in 60 minutes, or 98.7–104.0 in 50. Transfer overhead, verification, source inventory and the last piece's pipeline latency can raise these requirements.

These are necessary lower bounds, not this site's measured capacity. Rate a recovery using actual outstanding bytes by encoding, usable link payload/s, C2 work, already completed receipts and the measured overlapping pipeline. If a normal home restart requires someone to log in or double-click, display how much recovery time remains before clean release becomes infeasible. The owner sees separately whether the source holds everything, which batches already arrived, whether all C2 passed by expiry, and how much C4 can finish offline later. Rehearse a reconnect before and just after that measured boundary with provider expiry fixed.
