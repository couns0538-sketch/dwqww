### The finding of 03:36 (it changed the plan before any code)
My first plan checked an ENDED slot against the rows of the FINAL schedule. That would refuse a correct day: the
guard takes controls after the dispatch end (its state `dispatch_end`), a control's schedule may only shorten
bounds, and its extension races the workers' own clock. A worker ends at the dispatch end and seals the rows of the
schedule IT HOLDS; a start after its ended line does nothing. So a schedule can arrive after a slot has ended, and
its new rows are rows that slot never held. A range seal names the schedule it was written under, so:
- A1: the schedule a seal names (an int 0 to n; 0 = RUN.json's `pilot_ranges`) has a row of the seal's slot and
  round. Otherwise: "no range of round r in the schedule it names".
- A2: the seal's `first`, `last` and `short` equal that row's (canonical bytes). Otherwise: "not the schedule's range".
- A3: an ENDED slot has a range seal (plain or wire copy) of every row of its own in schedule L, the highest schedule
  its seals name (0 when none). Otherwise: "ranges/r....w....seal.json is missing (and no wire copy)".
- A note, not a refusal: rows of the FINAL schedule that an ended slot has no seal of, and that A3 does not require,
  are named on the output ("w001 ended without a range seal of round(s) [2]: ..."). The blind side is said there:
  their ids are not in the inventory.
- A0: every schedule's `ranges` and RUN.json's `pilot_ranges` pass the worker's own `check_rows` (a refusal is the
  worker's Stop 4, exit 4).
