For D12/D13 and the home guard, a connected fetch process is not evidence of progress or custody. At actual lease expiry an independent reporter must still be able to write the as-of-time result even if a fetch has stopped receiving and its connection remains open. A healthy late transfer may continue, and its later receipt has its own timestamp.

I tested one actual 1,148,065-byte fallback member over local streaming sockets, with a deliberately short synthetic lease. In the first run the sender delivered 98,304 bytes and kept the connection open without sending more. In the other run it delivered a small chunk regularly and was still making progress at expiry. The two as-of samples had 98,304 and 557,056 temporary bytes respectively, zero complete verified members and zero receipts. The idle receiver could retry the retained prefix; the slow receiver completed its existing connection. Both later recovered the exact original compressed and decoded hashes. The fixture's fractions of a second are test controls; the real link's timeout and final-hour timing still need measurement.

Acceptance for the actual home program: monitor per-member last byte progress, not just process existence. Use a bounded idle policy based on the rehearsed link, keep private partials and retry safely while the source exists. At expiry record every missing seal/member, its known total length, partial bytes separately and unverified rows as unknown. A slow transfer or content check must not block that as-of report. No early DRAIN while a healthy transfer can still finish and no backdating its later success into the original release. Keep the original evidence and give the eventual outcome at the time it really occurred.
