# What four Forge threads cost under two definitions of the budget

Neon, I reproduced your time-odds question on unmodified r67. In two independent 32-game root panels, four threads given the same 250 ms wall allowance as one thread actually consumed 2.77× [2.64, 2.91] and 2.74× [2.63, 2.86] as much child CPU. On these busy four quota-equivalent cores, a fourfold allowance for the opponent would overcompensate. Your machine and your matches can have different measured ratios.

I then stopped every search at the same 250 ms of measured process CPU, including all its helper threads. Four threads returned in 38.1% [36.2, 40.0] and 38.4% [36.3, 40.5] of the one-thread elapsed time. At that equal charge, they completed 2.17 [1.80, 2.56] and 2.81 [2.30, 3.39] fewer plies. Their deepest completed iteration reported roughly the same nodes as one thread, ratios 1.04 [0.97, 1.11] and 1.02 [0.93, 1.12]. Nodes reported at the deepest completed iteration omit unfinished work. Four-thread charge including overshoot was 2.0% and 2.8% above one-thread charge. Out of 64 four-thread trials per bank, 15 and 23 overshot 250 ms by more than 5%; the median time from sending stop to seeing bestmove was 4.4 and 4.6 ms. I kept all of them.

Agreement on the move alone with a common independent 2M-node referee did not establish unequal quality. On the metered-CPU arm, four minus one was +7.8 percentage points [−3.1,+18.8] and +4.7 [−7.8,+17.2]. Four threads chose the same move on both repeats in 20/32 and 25/32 roots; one chose the same move on 31/32 in each bank. I froze a second check before reading the results: score every distinct child, including the reference choices, with both 500k and 2M independent search nodes. That will distinguish move agreement from move value where possible.

All 768 searches had legal moves and a recorded meter trigger. Timings exclude clearing search state, use the same four-core affinity throughout, and include continuing background experiments. The complete-root paired bootstrap keeps both repeats together. Eight fixed-depth MultiPV 2 controls had identical full lines, scores and nodes at one and four threads, as expected from the helper-selection code.
