# Forge’s server day

**Wait before renting.** The three reviewers agree on how the day should work. Seven of the 27 published programs, tests and service files have all three approvals. The complete system still needs repairs, missing programs and an unpaid rehearsal with the home PC. Two billion usable positions remains a target. The proposed pair of machines has yet to demonstrate that rate. This report records the package published at shared revision `8e705146` and the reviews through Neon’s 220400Z message. [Agreed operating plan](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/8e705146c6369f523aae1146944e07f61f323b42/debate/server_day/SETUP_PLAN.md) · [Agreed file and recovery rules](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/8e705146c6369f523aae1146944e07f61f323b42/debate/server_day/FORMATS.md)

## How the day will run

1. **Prove the machines can do the job.** Before booking, run the complete reviewed package on a free machine and the home PC. Test the actual start-after-reboot path. On the selected server, check the resources the service can really use, build the frozen engine twice, and compare known games with the PC. An owner-supplied replacement starts a fresh run, passes those same checks and measures its own speed. If the old machine is unreachable, its work stays separately identified until it can be reconciled.
2. **Measure the whole route before committing the day.** Start with empty queues and a declared number of full rounds at the intended worker count, with the home PC doing its normal work. Record completed games per hour, positions per game including games that yielded none, and the fraction the PC actually kept. Convert each stage to kept positions per second. Use the lower of the measured whole-route rate and the slowest stage. An unfinished pilot gives no qualified forecast. Optional matches get only measured surplus time after preserving the owner’s chosen self-play margin.
3. **Play, transfer and check from the first batch.** The server seals small batches of complete games. It automatically offers compressed game data and compressed control records. The home PC fetches them, verifies their fingerprints, replays the moves, corrects results against endgame tables, filters positions and saves complete rounds. Only kept positions in durable rounds at home count toward two billion. Each stage records what arrived, what it processed, what is waiting and why. Retried deliveries earn no extra credit.
4. **Protect completed work.** Each worker owns one numbered sequence of games and records its progress before continuing. The server retains all sealed originals and compressed copies through the lease, including when the PC is offline. Low space pauses production. Independent queues let transfer continue during a temporary slowdown in checking or filtering, within the space reserved for each queue. After a proven same-machine reboot, the original deadline still applies. A restored folder alone cannot prove where a worker should resume.
5. **Reserve the last hour.** Production ends at the earlier of lease start plus 23 hours and lease end minus the time needed to close, with at least one full hour reserved. Stop dispatching games earlier by the longest measured game plus a margin. End every remaining search by the cutoff. The final hour seals, checks, transfers and accounts for data already produced. Report kept rows both at cutoff and after the drain, then list missing pieces, cleanup evidence and the provider’s release confirmation separately. Unresolved data and billing require the owner’s preselected policy. [Timing, release and substitute-machine decisions](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/8e705146c6369f523aae1146944e07f61f323b42/debate/server_day/SETUP_PLAN.md#3-decisions)

## What the numbers support

Two billion kept positions in 23 hours requires **24,155 kept positions per second**, rounded up. If setup consumes two hours with zero credit, 21 hours remain and the requirement rises to **26,456/s**. With a historical 122.14 kept positions per game, the first figure would require about 712,000 completed games per hour. The real forecast uses the new pilot’s games per hour and kept positions per game. If fewer positions fit, the script follows the owner’s choice to stop or continue at a reduced target.

The earlier loaded home PC trial played 2,000 games on one worker. Sixteen finished with zero rows. It kept 94.78% of raw positions and its older home software processed 42,332 raw positions per CPU-second through three steps. At that sample’s yield, normal compressed payload for two billion kept positions is about **9.14 GB** before retries. The server must also retain its originals. The corresponding space estimate is **167.6 GB** in the normal format or **197.3 GB** if every piece uses the measured Linux fallback, before temporary files, indexes, logs and reserves. The full-width paid server rate, current Windows checking rate, real link with retries and complete closing time still need measurement. [PC measurements, methods and limits](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/8e705146c6369f523aae1146944e07f61f323b42/debate/server_day/SETUP_PLAN.md#10-measurements-owed-each-gets-its-raw-file-and-a-line-here)

On this Linux machine, the inventory program took 96–98 seconds and about 303 MiB of memory to account for 16.384 million scheduled identities in synthetic game records. Verifying the existing inventory after a restart took another 101 seconds. The unpaid rehearsal must time the retry under load, including transfer and independent checking at home. [Full-size closing trial](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/23edd40072e25383b770fbd15232598457792a25/debate/msgs/20261123T220144Z_astra_full_day_inventory_cost_and_one_restart.md)

## What changed in the engine plan

The original order stands. Finish tuning and repair the training loop, calibrate the measuring tools and test whether progress really stalled (W1–W4). Rehearse the rental, check the training card, decide which rounds qualify, and validate the disk reader and width limits (W5–W9). Test the filter, teacher and training recipe separately, then train on two billion kept positions before widening the network (W10–W13). Test the first search changes as a group, retain legal-move and reference checks on every build, and leave the deferred experiments behind their stated conditions (W14–W16). [Original plan and amendments](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/bf47f95e103cebf82c9b70d420cccd324a4b5631/debate/FINAL_PLAN.md)

The first debate added ten checks. Count each match game once (A16), bind each generated game to its opening and the actual engine command (A23), and produce identical round bytes regardless of arrival order (A26). Check whether the training reader mixes games, reshuffles on every pass and detects an accidentally repeated pass (A17, A20, A22). Verify the actual training features, outputs and targets (A24), and stop when the intended network failed to load (N17). Measure where tuned settings take effect and compare the final setting against its starting value and both ends of the range (A18, N23). Eight later entries set conditions for deeper labels, a second training seed, difficult endgames, multi-core play, clock use, choice of opponents and openings, network shape and retuning search (A19, A21, A25, N18–N22). [Adopted and deferred changes](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/bf47f95e103cebf82c9b70d420cccd324a4b5631/debate/FINAL_PLAN.md#addendum-changes-agreed-by-astra-and-neon-after-the-debates-end)

In the new plan, A27 checks training ideas on whole games held out of training and with a separate scoring engine. A second change requires matching game history and search settings, or proof that reuse is harmless, before reusing a teacher’s score. Astra and Neon agreed to identical bytes at 200233Z and 201300Z respectively. Future trials have measurable starting points, including a stronger producer changing the training mix, a memory-limited reader, a measured multi-core bottleneck or frequent endgames where the existing tables lack answers. Each needs a priced experiment and the relevant playing test. [Engine plan approved by both seats](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/dfc0a0c2c114a20bcdfbdd1d4ecbdcfa50aad70c/debate/engine_plan/ENGINE_PLAN_8H.md) · [Both exact votes](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/a5531b079f0c9a1a13a1f6add2047746b0fa2fac/debate/msgs/20261123T201408Z_astra_canonical_revised_engine_plan_has_both_exact_votes.md)

Keep the current producer, opening policy and teacher. Across 768 finished games an external teacher failed to show a consistent playing advantage. Trying a second early opening move reduced distinct positions per CPU-hour by about 7.5%. A smaller training reader saved memory and resumed exactly, but 512 games left equal playing strength unresolved. Another 256 direct games showed that a close ranking inferred from a common opponent was still too uncertain to select a winner. Neon’s deeper-helper search scored 56.56% in 160 short-clock games combined. The later report gave 46.25% in 40 games on fresh openings at a longer clock. It stays an experiment pending a larger longer-clock test. Earlier failed tests included a larger producer hash, a sole-move shortcut, fifty-move damping, selectively deepening or averaging labels, mirrored labels, disabling pruning in bare-king searches, delaying exact moves as the halfmove clock advances, and the tested refresh and fusion optimizations.
