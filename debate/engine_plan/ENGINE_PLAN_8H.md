# Forge after the first two billion positions

Astra's working text for Neon's review, 23 November 2026. The three-seat baseline is [the ordered plan](../FINAL_PLAN.md). It contains R1–R6, W1–W16 and the previously adopted A16–A26 and N17–N23. The order remains: qualify the generator and trainer, verify the store, run the 768-wide comparison on the intended scale, then try 1024. A promising measure can earn an experiment. A net enters play only after its matched games. Changes to search go through R2 as a bundle. A recipe on which future training will depend gets A21's separate matched training seeds when its spending trigger is met.

## Addendum agreed on exact wording

Three local split audits took 3.50 CPU seconds; four native net passes with existing referee labels took 0.43 wall seconds. Price the full scan on the PC before booking any training. Neither local run establishes a gain or a defect in the owner's trainer.

## Later stages

### 1. Once the promoted producer or its labels change

Use A19 only after W8 qualifies, two billion kept positions are verified, and its producer or W13 trigger is met. Keep generation node budgets equal, account for actual positions and games, and measure generation CPU time, training card time and transfer separately. Compare shallow training on more positions against deeper training on fewer. If it is worth selecting positions for expensive labels, first check the proposed selector on independent game panels, a separate referee and a uniform draw at the same measured cost. A small integer score near zero alone failed. On 2,902 independent roots, spending the extra nodes on that quarter worsened the independent-label error by 1.46% on one half and 0.14% on the other. Its preregistered improvement was 5% on both halves and two references. Do not spend on training that selector on r67. A different signal requires a new experiment. A19 and the matched training cost survive this result. If two recipes emit different targets, apply A27 before treating their offline validation as comparable. [Selective-label experiment](../msgs/20261123T173331Z_astra_near_even_selective_labels_miss_bar.md) · [Four-network independent test](../msgs/20261123T174423Z_astra_common_referee_validation_on_four_native_nets.md)

**Recheck how games end when the producer changes.** Of Neon's 400 r67 games, 228 ended at the evaluation limit and 89 at draw adjudication. In 120 evaluation-limit cases a 128k re-search agreed with the adjudicated winner's sign. None of 89 allegedly drawn positions exceeded 200 cp at 128k. A deeper score from the same engine is a check for disagreement. A tablebase or a continuation to a board-rule ending is a stronger outcome check. Repeat on the next promoted net whose architecture or score scale changes. Start with at least 200 independently sampled games per relevant reason, preserve full histories and use a deliberately loose adjudication threshold as the failure control. With zero errors in 200, the one-sided 95% upper limit is 1.49%; a claim below 0.5% needs at least 598 independent zero-error cases. Inconclusive audits remain open. Report total games as well as the eligible subset. At 89/400, collecting 200 draw terminals takes about 899 new games on average before the deeper searches. Change the rule only after a real signal, with equal generation cost, W5 throughput and A19/R6/A21 games.

**Test exploration only when diversity falls.** On Neon's sample, 1,419 of 50,882 rows repeated a board placement and side to move. Count exact opening collisions, duplicates within a game, and duplicates across games on the real store. A proposed later trigger is a duplicate rate above 10% on a fresh round or at least 15% loss of distinct positions per paid core-hour to repeated trajectories. Check both against the verified two-billion baseline. Planting the same opening twice must be detected. Try one opening/exploration change at equal paid generation hours. A fixed mixed-depth generation recipe remains an option only after A19's direct 2k/8k comparison and a budget that covers two matched trainings per seed. State mix by games and the resulting share of CPU hours separately.

### 2. When one opponent or the opening set stops measuring progress

Use N20's internal trigger: a promoted net shows more capped or slower gates and a frozen known strength contrast loses at least 25% of its separation or needs twice the hours. For the outside ladder, use top unrestricted opponent score at least 0.75 and its paired lower bound above 0.70. Choose an opponent that restores a useful score range, freeze books, hashes and clock and run N20's four cells. Each opening supplies eight games across old/new Forge and old/new opponent. Keep all eight together when estimating whether the measured gain transfers. Cost is eight games per opening divided by measured end-to-end games/hour. A r67-era test with three opponents needed 64 more 250k-node games to supply a missing direct pairing, costing 814 engine CPU seconds for that edge here. Estimated indirect errors by training seed were 0.00 and +9.38 score points; intervals were [−14.06,+13.98] and [−1.52,+23.31]. They cannot support substituting the inferred result for the missing games. [Direct games and clock evidence](../msgs/20261123T174704Z_astra_verify_clock_contract_and_direct_net_triangle.md)

If a working gate contains many closely related openings, identify each parent and count reversed pairs by family. Compare the uncertainty under parent resampling and ordinary paired uncertainty on two held-out control panels. The proposed trigger for a redesigned sample is a ratio of at least 1.20 on both panels with a known contrast and demonstrable shared families in the actual PC suite. In 2,048 native calibration games on two banks, four eight-ply children per parent gave ratios 0.960, 1.001, 1.045 and 0.949 across two contrasts. Literal copies did raise the ratio to 2.012. Keep the ordinary gate today. Cost of the diagnostic here was 7.8 engine core-minutes, with shallow games on synthetic openings. The PC's book needs its own data and timing. [Opening-family controls](../msgs/20261123T173927Z_astra_opening_families_native_uncertainty_result.md)

**Repeated candidate selection.** If at least three candidates have been compared on a reused panel and the PC's own null replay shows that its selection rule exceeds the stated false-acceptance rate, freeze the winning bytes before a fresh confirmation. Record all candidates, panel identities, overlaps and stopping rules; retain R2/W13. In 1,152 native games at 12k nodes, the best of six on bank A scored 55.21% there and 48.96% on independent bank B. The B winner scored 52.60% there and 50.52% on A. In a modeled correlated sign-flip null, the nominal one-sided screen flagged the selected candidate on the selection bank 23–25% of the time, and about 5% on a separate bank. With the clearly weak sixth arm removed the corresponding figures were 21% versus 5%. This is a diagnostic for selection. Forge's actual sequential-error rate requires its own replay. The 1,152-game study cost 15.2 local engine core-minutes. Price a real confirmation from timed games/hour before adding any extra match.

### 3. When the stronger evaluation changes what search does

Finish W1 and W14 and promote a stronger net first. Under N22, compare the same 600 fresh searches at final settings and budget, with counters collected by one profiled build. Investigate deeper switches only where a repeatable activity change survives the N22 threshold. Neon's 20-root probe saw `LmrSignedCorrection` affect 0, 1 and 6 roots at depths 10, 13 and 15; `CaptureFutilityBase=120` reduced nodes at depth 15 by about 12%. Neither fixed-depth node count nor number of move changes measures strength. Before a deeper tune, measure games per hour at both budgets on the actual host, set a CPU-hour limit, freeze independent confirmation openings, and retain R2, N22's deep confirmation, R3 for changed speed and R1 before self-play. A dormant option can remain off. [Neon's pulse](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/331ec302688c93ce8ca06b014a52646183c892ed/debate/msgs/20261123T173611Z_neon_phase1_workstreams_and_a7e1_review.md)

The capture-only quiescence search occasionally prints a line just short of quiet checkmate. Across two 8,192-game cohorts only one actual 2k move allowed an immediate mate available only by a quiet reply. In that position a stronger independent search already reported a forced loss. Free r67 selected none of the planted losing moves on 320 exposed roots at depth two or at ordinary node budgets. A change inside search must first show a plausible root-decision opportunity on a promoted engine. Beginning with the 600-root activity check, a further deterministic play-through can count the first differing move or termination per opening. At equal nodes, zero divergences in 1,024 independently sampled games give a one-sided 95% optimistic upper about +2.03 Elo near equality. One-color and reversed-pair screening cost about 8.95 and 17.91 local CPU-hours respectively before early exits. A passed opportunity screen only earns R2. Keep the current implementation until a material effect is observed. [Quiet-mate controls](../msgs/20261123T175236Z_astra_quiet_mate_horizon_root_opportunity.md)

### 4. When strength on the clock, or on many cores, is the target

After N19's stronger-net and clock-play prerequisites, confirm the behavior of each supported clock before tuning. Record elapsed time, planned soft/hard times, deadline observation, final move and forfeits separately on sudden death, increment and moves-to-go. With 2 s plus 1 s increment, overhead 10 ms and one move to go, the allocator returned 1,592 ms after changing four sudden-death settings. Its source explicitly distinguishes both clock schemes. In an independent one-legal-move case node share halved a 529 ms soft basis at depth one and bestmove arrived after 116–184 ms. The main search polls a separate hard limit. An optional combined soft ceiling or a changed classical-clock policy needs a specified contract, fixtures and clock games. A 15 cp fall already adds 15% to that iteration at the default setting. Changes for cumulative falls or mate transitions need their own controls. Price each arm from completed games/hour at its tested control and enforce N19's separate held-out clock and forfeit bars. [Native clock reproduction](../msgs/20261123T174704Z_astra_verify_clock_contract_and_direct_net_triangle.md)

After N18's correctness repairs, measure fixed-clock and fixed-depth scaling at 1, 2, 4 and the available 8 or 16 threads. Hold affinity, cores, hash, build and openings constant and measure CPU per move, wall time to depth and winning score. Neon's smallest candidate preserves the striped locks and avoids an unconditional write to the shared flag on every ordinary table access. At four threads on eight openings an interleaved native test measured 6.562M versus 6.980M nodes/s (+6.4%). One-thread bench stayed at 226,187 nodes and a sanitizer run reported zero warnings on its exercised paths. Astra's independent hot-hit test confirmed a shared-table cost even on disjoint stripes. An isolated shared-flag control cost about four times the CPU per operation at four threads relative to independent flag cache lines. Those figures measure different workloads.

Run the minimal candidate on the target PC only when multi-thread clock play is planned. A proposed threshold for a clock match is at least +3% NPS on an interleaved target-host pilot, followed by improved time to depth, clean lifecycle tests and a positive held-out same-clock result. Require all readers, diagnostic accessors and prefetches to quiesce before resize, clear or destruction. Measure the standing worker pool, helper diversity and move voting separately. A different table representation is a later hypothesis if, after those changes, eight-thread per-core efficiency remains below 75% and profiling shows substantial lock cost. An unsynchronized run is unsuitable as a strength or correctness reference. Pilot cost is positions × repetitions × clock duration; 4,000 games at 30 s/game and four truly concurrent games would be 8.33 wall-hours, a forecast until the actual host is timed. [Native lock and clock measurements](https://github.com/couns0538-sketch/dwqww/blob/7987662c6ccdf0f21c83800acd1cad088baa8954/debate/msgs/20261123T175648Z_neon_agree_a27_and_six_later_stage_entries.md) · [Independent table controls](../msgs/20261123T180116Z_astra_six_stage_units_uncertainty_and_tt_controls.md)

### 5. When the training window, network shape or tablebase changes the bottleneck

Measure examples actually used per second, cold-read and data-wait time, card utilization, memory high-water marks, and card-hours per completed recipe. Distinguish a kept position from an example the trainer uses. At 91% usage and 50.3 bytes/used row, 2/4/10 billion kept positions occupy about 92/183/458 decimal GB. If all are used, they occupy 101/201/503 GB. At the reported 5.32M to 4.445M used examples/s and 40 passes, the corresponding compute-only times are 3.8–4.6 / 7.6–9.1 / 19.0–22.7 hours at 91%, or 4.2–5.0 / 8.4–10.0 / 20.9–25.0 at 100%. Measure setup, cold first pass, checkpoints and export separately. Separate archive compression and trainer shard representations have different sizes.

1. If host memory exceeds a proposed 75%, swapping starts, or unpacking threatens W8's 30 GB reserve, test a two-level shuffle and bounded disk feeder. Re-run A17/A20/A22 on the new buffer and an intentionally sequential reader. Time the actual cold path.
2. If data waiting exceeds a proposed 15% of step time, fix input first. If the card is at least 85% busy and a single run exceeds the owner's turnaround budget, qualify two cards before four. Preserve global batch, row coverage, optimizer schedule and total work. Check a deliberately duplicated rank, dropped tail and a rank with no valid examples; reduce the weighted sums across ranks. Predeclare a tolerance from repeated reference updates and report both gradient and per-weight-group update differences (W6). Proposed minimum scaling efficiency is 80% on the measured 2–4 card stage before considering eight. R5 qualifies an equivalent reader or trainer. An altered batch or recipe must pass R6/A21. Price GPU-hours as card count × measured wall-hours, including communication and input stalls.
3. If the newest promoted producer has at least a measured +50 Elo over the oldest active generation and contributes at least 25% of the window (proposed policy), test one recency weighting or eviction policy against flat pooling. Match global steps, card budget, window counts and seeds. Use A27 on later games, then A21's matched games. A slow reader and stale training data are separate hypotheses.

Once N21's verified 2–4-billion-row window and W13's 1024 result exist, test one of bucket count, width or output architecture at a time. Count examples per bucket and compare exact scalar and supported vector outputs on both extreme signs before training. At widths above 1024, resolve AVX2 lane overflow and measure per-worker memory. Use identical window, budget and training seeds and the target PC clocks at 10+0.1 and 60+0.6. A narrower surrogate is relevant if the stronger network's inference cost becomes a measured bottleneck. A past post-training 768-to-640 version missed its 12 cp agreement, 1% referee degradation and 5% throughput bars. Refitting the 640-wide output weights on 32,600 independent calibration roots reduced teacher error to 23.25 cp and left 500k-referee error 2.70% worse [1.77%, 3.66%]. On 128 openings played twice with alternating arm order, compact and padded versions had identical transcripts, while the smaller model gained 3.62% wall throughput [2.29%, 5.02%]. All three preregistered bars still failed. The refit itself took 1.37 CPU seconds, excluding preparation, validation and play. Price a full training from examples per second on each proposed shape. [Existing shape and cost evidence](../FINAL_PLAN.md#for-later-once-the-engine-is-stronger)

Keep three endgame decisions separate. For tournament root or in-search probes, require the promoted engine, an actual clock target and measured lost conversions; a proposed first threshold is at least 2% of a frozen 2,000-game clock cohort reaching five men, including losses a rule-aware table contradicts. Compare the existing restricted root evaluator wherever A25 applies. Test unsupported/missing files, castling, known cursed wins, halfmove clocks, and repetition histories at equal clocks. For six-man in-search probes, first qualify the smaller set, measure sufficient local storage and at least 5% six-man exposure in the intended long games. Sweep probe depth and concurrency on the target disk. Record cold/warm latency, hits, time forfeits and general as well as endgame-enriched paired scores. An enriched suite alone cannot qualify tournament play.

Offline, the 400-game pilot had 8.44% of rows at up to five men and 12.36% up to six. The *additional* six-man-only slice was about 3.92%, or 78.4M of two billion if that mix held. Count covered material classes, eligible histories and missing successor tables before quoting a full-scan budget. Compare unchanged five-man labels against a six-man-labeled copy, retaining raw search scores. A proposed trigger is at least 0.5% of games changing rule-aware outcomes beyond five-man coverage, or at least 1% wrong-sign six-man labels on a declared applicable cohort. Price by a 100k eligible-position cold/warm disk pilot and separately price A21's matched trainings and games. Tablebases that help tournament search and tables that improve training labels must each earn their own test.
