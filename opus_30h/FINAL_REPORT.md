### What this job learned about nets (for the next net job)
1. The lock net's function can be written EXACTLY into a bucketed v2 net (identity to d22 for KB1 / KB4 / KB8 maps,
   OB1 / OB8) and fine-tuned from there on the CPU: 3.5e8 positions in ~2 h 20 min on one CPU (torch, 1 thread).
2. More first-layer capacity (4 king buckets, same 256 hidden units) lowers the 5k-node-label holdout loss a lot and
   broadly: S31 b0.6 -5.6e-5 at 3.5e8 positions (n2e), -1.13e-4 at 1.37e9 (n2L); every material / king / label group
   improves; bucket seams stay small (<= 2.5 cp); but in games the v2 exe searches 0.12-0.15 ply less than the lock at equal time (all three matches, including the identical-evaluation control: a speed cost of the exe, under investigation).
3. Every v2 match must be read against a CONTROL: the gate exe playing the lock net itself (identical evaluation and
   search tree) scored -32.2 +/- 18.5 vs the lock exe - the v2 exe costs Elo on its own. Against that control the
   nets read n2L +22.6 +/- 26.6 and n2e +7.0 +/- 26.2: noise-compatible, but ordered as the S31 holdout predicted
   (more S31 gain, better play), while the deeper-label set (jobA) went the other way (n2L's jobA was the worse one).
   An interim reading of this job ("S31 does not predict play; jobA is the compass"), made before the control, is
   withdrawn - it is kept in a/RESULTS.md with its correction.
4. So the next lever is the EXE: the v2 path searches 0.11-0.13 ply less than the lock in games (in all four matches,
   control included); its cost has to go before a v2 net can win a gate. Labels stay a lever for later (the 50M
   relabel), but this job's evidence does not single them out.
5. The engine side is ready for that work: the v2 path (parity x3, walk 1.1M plies on a trained net, in-search verify,
   18-way loader fuzz, UCI conformance, sanitizer CI), the KB8 map (Python + Rust + exporter), the GPU trainer.
